<|det|>text [71, 321, 902, 819]<|/det|>At the same time to-day is also the first academic service of this term. Let us submit all that it brings, together with the work which we shall be called on to do, to the sway of this message of Jesus; namely that we are unworthy servants who, in whatever we may accomplish, are only fulfilling our obligations, and who in the spirit of patience are prepared for whatever God may send or bestow. We are so much the more determined to tread this path of self-surrender because we are also thinking of our friends who have been torn away from their work, whether as teachers or students, who are now out there at the front and who have suffered and must go on suffering unspeakable things. Whatever problems and difficulties oppress us here are nothing as compared with the cruel destiny which weighs on them. How could they steadfastly face this destiny if not in the spirit of straightforward unquestioning self-surrender, which renounces every right and claim and humbly and trustingly leaves it to God to decide for what service He needs man and what fate He will send man? And how could we maintain our inner spiritual communion with them if we were not at one with them in the determination to bow in calmness and trust to the will of God our Creator?
