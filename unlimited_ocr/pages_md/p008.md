Man who subjugates the threatening world more and more to his own purposes, seeks also to integrate into the structure of his ordered world that mystery which once peered at him with a thousand eyes out of the surrounding darkness and lurked around his footsteps. He seeks to give it a place which, though still imparting awe and fear, shines with the clear light of daily life and becomes the centre of a regularized cult. In fact, in proportion as man devotes himself with efficiency and enthusiasm to such ordered worship, his primal fear is benumbed and the mystery of his existence concealed by his religion.
<|det|>text [108, 307, 932, 419]<|/det|>This is illustrated by the story of the very place on which Paul delivered his discourse, for it was the shrine at which those dread demons of blood vengeance, the Erinnyes, were worshipped. Yet their cult resulted in their being given the name of the Eumenides, the gracious ones.
<|det|>text [108, 420, 932, 600]<|/det|>This then is the superstitious aspect of heathen religion; it breathes, not the true fear of the living God, but deep-rooted anxiety in face of the strange mystery of the world, and such religion spells also the attempt to master this anxiety through a human effort to secure control of the mystery and to integrate it with the norm of human life. Thus man tries to calm his anxious mind and to attain a secure mastery of his existence.
