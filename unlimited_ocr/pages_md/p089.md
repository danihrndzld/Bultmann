<|det|>text [48, 410, 865, 838]<|/det|>In this hour of evening worship we must allow a word of the eternal God to strike home to us. Which word shall it be? How are we to choose among the riches of the words of Scripture, without running the risk of selecting a word which is in harmony with our mood, and with those secret wishes hidden in our mood? The New Testament contains no word which has a direct bearing on a situation such as is ours to-day; it contains no word which alludes to specific turning points of time, whether those which nature has fixed such as morning and evening, or the course of the year and its seasons; or the beginning and end of those periods into which time is divided for the organization of our human work. The New Testament pays no attention to these time periods which are so significant to us. It knows only one critical turning point of time—when the “time was fulfilled”; it knows only one “now” which in human life is lifted above the stream of time—the “now” when the word of God strikes a man and which becomes for him the decisive moment between time and eternity.
