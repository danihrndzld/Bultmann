<|det|>text [111, 38, 937, 626]<|/det|>People whose inner life is empty may of course very easily fit into an organization, but they are incapable of genuine love and fellowship. The sign of our being something of value within is our capacity for receiving and giving love. A poet, Wilhelm Busch, with his vein of playful fantasy, describes with somewhat grim humour a dream which transported him into mathematical worlds. There he meets, instead of human beings, mathematical figures who, inwardly hollow, yet live a ghostly life like that of men. Thus with mocking irony he holds up a mirror to our human world, challenging men to reflect and reconsider. Is not our life as a whole threatened by the danger of being more and more absorbed in the organization, instead of being part of a genuine community of true persons who mean something intrinsically and so also can signify something for other people? Modern life increasingly crowds out times of pause for self-communion, for silent meditative heart-searching in face of eternity. Many people are overcome by boredom when the rush of activity ceases, whether it be that of work or that of pleasure; and few men can any longer bear to be alone. Solitude seems to them tedious. And is it worse in the eyes of God to steal or to transgress a moral commandment, than to grow bored? Yes, there are people who dread being alone and take refuge in a round of activities which give them no time for thought. They escape from themselves in sheer noise which stupefies their ability to think.
