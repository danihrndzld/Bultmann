Just now we will only say, quite briefly: the gospel spells the rejection of the world of the spirit if man sees in that world a structure which he himself has raised, the expression of his own creative power and perfection. But the gospel signifies the confirmation of the world of the spirit, if man recognizes in that world the divine authority under which he stands, and if he does not attempt to hide the fact that he can never satisfy the claim of this authority. And this not only in the sense of his realizing that he is never yet what he should be, but also in the sense of his realizing that he is always already what he should not be. He is guilty, not only in the sense that ever increasing obligations exceed the measure of his fulfilment, but also in the sense that he has rendered himself guilty by showing himself to be unworthy of these high duties. He is constantly frustrated not only by the imperfection of all things earthly in which the ideal can never find pure expression, but also by his own doing, by his own past which makes him unclean in the face of the holy. His conscience accuses him. He belongs to two worlds: his true and better self belongs to the world of the spirit, but that fact may not and cannot justify him or quieten the accusations of his conscience so long as he is bound to confess that this his true self has not really become his own but ever hovers before him as an unattained ideal. His empirical ego is in fact the battle-ground of the struggle between the powers of the spirit and the lower impulses. His ego is rent in twain; it lacks unity and purity. It needs cleansing, purification from the stains of the old man, emancipation from its disharmonies, regeneration; in a word, it needs divine grace. And it is just this which has become luminous for Paul in the face of Jesus Christ: the grace of God which has made him a new creature. "For it is the God who said, 'Let light shine out of darkness', who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Christ."
