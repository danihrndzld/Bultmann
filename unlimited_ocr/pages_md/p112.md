It is just this which we feel and dimly suspect when we speak of our real self and its fulfilment. But have we the will to carry through this feeling? Are we really ready, when the beyond, when God confronts us, to surrender the world? Hence are we truly ready, when God confronts us in the figure of Jesus, to become His disciples and to allow the world to be simply the world? This is the choice which is in question here. This is the point on which we are called to make a clear-sighted decision, namely that what at times we probably dimly surmise, what at times we no doubt long for—that it should really become the controlling force in our lives. Here is the decisive issue which Jesus puts before men. The light which He sheds is the light of the eternal world; the joy which He bestows is supernatural joy. Truly to desire this light and this joy, hence to be His disciple, means to live as though one no longer really belonged to this world. Do we desire this with all our hearts?
<|det|>text [114, 466, 939, 873]<|/det|>That restless unappeasable surmise that our true self cannot find its fulfilment in this world, in this-worldly values and aims, gifts and duties, usually comes over us only when we disastrously fail in our desires and strivings, when that to which we had lost our hearts, that which made our little world dear and familiar and homely to us, is shattered. Or again it visits us when a sinister future looms threateningly before us, for which we feel we are no match, and in whose sombre shadows the colours of the present fade. At such moments as these when the world in which we live suddenly strikes us as alien, when in the midst of its homelessness we long for our true home, we are visited by this intimation of the beyond. Then it may well be that we feel as if the affairs of this world in which we are so deeply implicated were a confusing dream from which we must awake.
