And he comes to experience the joy that is granted him when he uses the power of thought and insight not only in the service of the practical ends of life, but also and above all when he uses and trains them to grasp the truth, to understand the world as it really is and himself as he really is. <|det|>text [73, 266, 889, 693]<|/det|>But man lives at the same time in the visible world which is ruled by sheer hunger and brutal power. Nature seems to conspire against the world of the spirit. Material needs, want and sickness, rob the spirit of its freedom and subdue it to the world of the physical. The question as to what is good is superseded by the question as to what is useful. The understanding of truth and knowledge is exploited by the technology of practical life aims. The hours of uplifting joy in the contemplation of beauty grew few, and art no longer serves the delight and strengthening of the spirit but is prostituted in the service of entertainment and distraction. And we must go further and say that human society itself seems to be hostile to the life of the spirit. Right cannot be maintained without power, and how often does power come before right! How much better fare those people who are concerned with questions of the profitable rather than the good! So often does the lie triumph over truth, ambition over the tranquil surrender to the recognition of what is true and the ideal of beauty.
<|det|>text [69, 695, 889, 897]<|/det|>Thus man is constantly pressed by the temptation to abandon that life in two worlds which so often tears his breast and instead to follow purely practical life aims. He is faced by the decision: is he to yield to the absolute obligations with which the world of the spirit confronts him and in its service to shoulder a double burden of torment and trial?
