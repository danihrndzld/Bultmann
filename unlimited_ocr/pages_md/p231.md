But if anyone really supposes that there is a way to God which does not pass through the experience of utter tranquillization and submergence of which Luther speaks, that there is any man whom God does not constantly remind, through experience, that he is essentially nothing, then we must answer: such a one is only cherishing illusions; he entirely fails to see the realities of life; and even though his own life may pass outwardly without storm and stress, he is blind to what God is ever showing him if only he will see it. Is there after all any life without disillusionment and suffering? And can a so-called happy man, in whose life the measure of suffering is trifling, close his eyes to the suffering of others? must he not also suffer under the afflictions which strike down his fellow men? And does not death face us all, casting its shadow before it? No, if anyone imagines that there is a way to the living God which does not pass through the heart of darkness, then without realizing it he is already involved in the deepest darkness from which there is no issue—unless it be that he comes to realize that he is treading the way of darkness.
