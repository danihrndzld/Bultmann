In order to understand this we must remember that what is in view here is not any worldly or political issue but the ultimate issue, that of eternity, that of God. And here in the decisive question of God it is always better to decide against God than to be poised indecisively in a middle term. For the struggle against God implies always a certain degree of connexion with God; every struggle against God is a wrestling with God, as the example of one of the most passionate fighters against God, Friedrich Nietzsche, can show.
<|det|>text [106, 269, 930, 672]<|/det|>It must of course be a genuine and honest struggle against God. A struggle which understands the question of God only in theoretical terms or only as a political issue is not that. Such an attitude is in fact an ignoring, a silencing of the profound and searching question of the reality of God. And such a fighter is the half-hearted indecisive person for whom the question of the reality of God in all its seriousness and depth has not yet become vivid. But the passionate fighter against God stands near to God and his ear is open to the voice of eternity. Mere talk about God and religious belief does not of course bring us near to the living majesty of God. A man may talk a great deal about God and yet belong to the category of these half-hearted persons; for the search for God is only genuine when it stirs and masters man's mind as a passionate concern. Are we the kind of men for whom the question of God is a passionate concern? Or have we squandered our inner strength on other questions?
