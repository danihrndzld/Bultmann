<|det|>text [111, 106, 931, 628]<|/det|>What is the really superstitious element in this polytheistic worship? Might it for example be merely this, namely, that heathen worship was based on false conceptions, springing from a childish mode of thought, whereas Christianity, with its teaching about the unity of God, brought enlightenment and swept away all such false conceptions by its essential truth and maturity of thought? Certainly not. Paul could not have meant this, for the simple reason that he knew that Greek culture had long ago transcended the stage of polytheism, and that Greek philosophers taught, like Christianity, the unity of the God who is immanent in the world, in whom “we live and move and have our being”, and to whom man is related in the depths of his inner life. In fact he quotes a line from one of the Greek philosophical poets, “For we are indeed His offspring”, which implies the offspring of the one God. Those philosophers taught that the gods who are worshipped in the various temples according to ancient custom are but energies and emanations flowing from the primal Godhead which is everywhere worshipped under a diversity of names. Hence the struggle of Christianity against idolatry did not consist in the fact that it had to remove, by its superior enlightenment, false heathenish notions of God.
