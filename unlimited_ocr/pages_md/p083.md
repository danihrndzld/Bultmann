<|det|>text [62, 263, 881, 510]<|/det|>"Take my yoke upon you and learn from me." If the promise "I will refresh you" is to be valid for us, then of course we must submit ourselves to the law of Christ and take upon us His yoke. He says to us: "My yoke is easy and my burden is light." But only he experiences this who really takes upon him the yoke of Christ. For the truth is that those who do not at all feel themselves to be weary and heavy laden, recoil from taking the yoke of Christ and look upon it as harsh and crushing. The real meaning and blessing of Sunday is concealed from such people. They too celebrate Sunday but they misunderstand and abuse it.
<|det|>text [62, 512, 881, 895]<|/det|>We are of course aware that there are many people for whom the work in which they are engaged during the week does not constitute the meaning of their lives. They are not absorbed in their work. Their work remains something external to themselves; it is not for them a fulfilment of life but only a means of livelihood; and no doubt they feel their work to be a burden. For the real essence of life is for them the excitement of pleasure. There is no need to dwell on this misuse of Sunday which makes of Sunday not the Lord's Day, not a time of quiet and worship, but a time of noisy activity; not rest but sheer pleasure-seeking, not a time for self-recollection but a time for escaping from oneself, not an opportunity for inner concentration of being, but an opportunity for distraction and amusement. Such people not only miss the real spiritual blessing of Sunday but also for the most part the gift which it brings of outward quiet; and Monday morning finds them prostrated and peevish.
