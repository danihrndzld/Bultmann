If we warn people thus, how often are we told: “Yes, it is all very fine for you to talk! You are not so badly placed as I!” I think now of the many among us who have lost their jobs and their money. Who could fail to think of them with compassion and not understand their natural impatience? And yet for them too the answer is: patience! Not as a friendly human warning, for then they might justifiably retort: “It is easy for you to talk!” But it is the warning of God’s word itself: you must try to bear patiently the load placed on your shoulders.
<|det|>text [86, 296, 908, 789]<|/det|>Impatience only makes things worse and leads you to embittered and foolish thoughts. Impatience is harmful too for the community. How much anger and abuse of others arise from our impatience! On all sides we hear the complaint: “Nothing has become any better than it was formerly! In fact we were better off then than now! then there was more to eat and drink, more tobacco to smoke!” What blindness of impatient folly! As though things could anyhow be other than they are! As though we had not to bear the necessary consequences of our madness and delusions, and bear them bravely so that we might go forward again! In queues, in the bustle of business life and in offices, in the trams—how often is there a lack of patience and courtesy, and in consequence what mutual recriminations, in a spirit of bitterness and hatred! And how many people show an ungrateful and loveless insensibility to the far greater burdens and misery of others! Moreover how much better off are we than those unfortunate ones who, driven from their homes, are seeking refuge with us from East Germany! No doubt we are involved in difficulties in attempting to provide them with shelter, but only patience will enable us to deal effectively with the situation.
