<|det|>text [102, 94, 929, 709]<|/det|>The modern man who no longer builds an altar to the unknown God has either forgotten God completely and cast aside the thought of God, or else he has a god who is only too well known. Just as Paul takes for his starting point the inscription on the altar, so also he makes use of the philosophical theism entertained in cultured circles, of the outlook which apprehends God as Him in whom we “live and move and have our being” and which can proudly affirm: “For we are indeed His offspring.” This was the teaching of the stoics: the Deity inheres in, flows through, and moulds all things; it breathes in our breath and courses in our blood. In this sense the modern man too gladly accepts the statement: “In Him we live and move and have our being.” And this truth moves him like ancient man to devotion and kindles him to worship the divine energies of the world. The theme becomes a solemn and soothing musical accompaniment to his life, and he does not notice that in this way, by his very religion, he is resisting God and relegating Him to a remote distance. For this kind of religion, which proposes to heap upon man all riches and abundance of life and to set him in the intricate pattern of a highly organized world, also wishes to regard the world as a beautiful work of art and man’s veneration for the divine as an integral part of this living All. In fact it uses man’s veneration for the life-forces which flow through the universe as a means of justifying the stirrings and strivings of the human will, and it speaks about the god within man’s own breast.
<|det|>text [102, 710, 931, 916]<|/det|>But it does not wish to recognize the true God who confronts man not as a confirmation of his self-centred being but as a critical force; the living God who stands over against man and questions him in gift and fate, the Creator-God before whom man is as nothing, the Judge to whom man is accountable. God is not the unfateful current of power which streams through the life of nature, but the Lord of history.
