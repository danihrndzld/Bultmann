<|det|>text [58, 35, 881, 171]<|/det|>For it is the curse of a life which is so feverishly hurried that the pauses which do occasionally arise are void of content. At such times a terrible boredom may overcome him; he may suddenly be seized by a questioning as to the meaning of his life and feel mounting within him a disgust which can even lead to suicide.
<|det|>text [58, 174, 881, 514]<|/det|>"In Him we live and move and have our being." Whoever lives with the feeling of emptiness and tedium has ceased to realize this truth. If he is courageous enough to reflect, he will of course become aware that it is precisely in the sense of void which oppresses him that God is speaking to him and asking him questions: questioning him about himself and calling him to account as to what he has made of his life. But can man face such questions? The organization of life has discovered the means of enabling man to deal with these moments of emptiness and tedium: amusement and distraction, pleasure and noise! Man is to forget the shattering truth: "In Him we live and move and have our being!" But what is operative in this frenzied effort to become absorbed in bustle and noise but that original fear of life which man seeks so desperately to still?
<|det|>text [58, 516, 881, 629]<|/det|>Technology, which was intended to subjugate the energies of nature and harness to his service the forces which man once felt to be daemonic and crushing, has itself become a daemonic power compelling man to serve itself and thus bringing him to lose his own soul.
<|det|>text [58, 631, 881, 903]<|/det|>It would of course be a foolish mistake to try to wage war against the technical resources which we all need for our lives. It is rather a question of making it clear to ourselves that technics can be used for both good and evil, and that it confronts us with a powerful temptation. Against this temptation we must fight; if we succumb to it, then technology assumes the character of the daemonic and robs us of our true life in God. The temptation is that the very success of technology may lead us to believe that our life is secure and to lose the sense of its frailty; to regard ourselves as master of our life and to benumb man's original fear of life instead of allowing the latter to drive him on to seek his God.
