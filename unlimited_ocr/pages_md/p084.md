<|det|>text [106, 39, 934, 265]<|/det|>But both sets of people, those who are entirely absorbed in their work and for whom Sunday is merely a day of outward relaxation, and the others who are indifferent to their work, and who misuse Sunday in chasing the pleasures of the world, are not always to be so clearly separated. Many men, in fact most, have something of both attitudes in themselves and sometimes behave in one way, sometimes in the other. Not one of us does not stand in danger of misusing Sunday and forgetting that it is the Lord's Day whose yoke we should take upon us and from whom we should learn.
<|det|>text [106, 266, 934, 764]<|/det|>There were once times, and even to-day there are still many places, where the Sabbath rest was most strictly carried out and where any kind of amusement or pleasure on Sunday was considered an abuse. The danger often is indeed that the easy yoke of the Lord may become oppressive; in which case the freedom for which it is the purpose of this day to liberate us, turns into a form of slavery. But for the majority of us, generally speaking, this danger is not great; on the contrary! And we should do our best to maintain sound and ancient Sunday customs. The latter of course seem harsh and oppressive to those who have not grasped the true meaning of Sunday. And the chief point is that we should keep alive the sense of this meaning and purpose among our people, or revive it if need be; that we should try to instil an understanding of the truth that our life should not be wholly taken up in preoccupation with the things of time and space, whether it be a question of work or pleasure; that our souls belong to a higher world, to God's world. Then even Sunday customs which formerly perhaps may have seemed harsh and enslaving, will become a light burden and we shall be grateful to them because they have disciplined us and enabled us to taste to the full the healing blessings of Sunday.
