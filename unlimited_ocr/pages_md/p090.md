<|det|>text [113, 179, 941, 383]<|/det|>Nevertheless, it is sensible to look for a particular word of Scripture; for the one word is enshrined in many forms. And if this evening we seek such a word—we who are specially conscious now of the passing of time—then let it be a word which assures us, whose time is now fading away, of eternity lying above and beyond time; let it be a word which will bring the peace of God into the dispeace and agitation of our human time. Let us listen to the farewell words of Jesus to His disciples:
<|det|>text [113, 384, 937, 452]<|/det|>"Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you. Not as the world gives, give I to you. Let not your hearts be troubled, neither let them be afraid" (John 14:27).
<|det|>text [113, 453, 941, 610]<|/det|>It is peace which we need, peace which gives us the certitude that we are not delivered over to the irresistible sweep of time but are already integrally rooted in eternity. But it is the peace of the Lord which the world cannot give; His peace which is essentially other than all contentment which the world can give. And does not the end of the term make us acutely aware that we need this peace?
