<|det|>text [58, 190, 885, 642]<|/det|>This is the characteristic judgment of modern man, voiced again and again, and to-day especially many people are speaking like this. There is no need for me to go into details. In fact, both the parable of Jesus and ruthless fate give us the same shock of offence repeatedly: it is not at all obvious to man's perception that a God of justice and goodness reigns supreme. The God of the parable is the sovereign Lord to whose caprice man is surrendered, and in relation to whom he has no rights or claims—and this too is the God who does violence to man's conscience in the tragic mysteries of human destiny. In both cases it is the same shock of offence! The only difference is that the man of to-day draws the conclusion that such a God is no God—whether he ends in indifference and resignation, or whether he draws the further conclusion that it is for human beings to take their lives in this world into their own hands in disregard of God. Let us make ourselves the masters, they say, we who are free and steadfast in purpose! In any case, man does not really surmount the obstacle implied in the offence that divine justice is not perceptible, divine goodness is not demonstrable.
