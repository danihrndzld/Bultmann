But inasmuch as Advent proclaims not only His ever expected future coming, to which we look forward in every distress and sore temptation, but proclaims also His having come in the Holy Nativity, it draws our attention at the same time to that unique miracle which summons us to decision. For the Holy Nativity of our Saviour is a miracle in the twofold sense that at this point there is proclaimed loud and clear to the poor the good news of the eternal light which transforms the appearance of the world, and also in that through this Word the flame of divine love is once more kindled among us.
<|det|>text [65, 286, 893, 648]<|/det|>For the Incarnation is a sheer miracle. The feast of Christmas which celebrates it has as a result lost just that sense which in pagan times characterized the festival of the winter solstice. The Christian feast is precisely not a festival growing out of the regular rhythm of nature and human life which is so closely intertwined with nature. Rather as a Christian feast it is torn out of nature's continuity and becomes the celebration of the miracle that to the poor of this world the good news is preached. No doubt this Christian feast is so deeply rooted in our whole way of life that it is also accompanied by much natural love and kindness which is not awakened by the Word. So much the more, for us Christians, this Word of the Gospel should stand right at the heart of the feast, penetrating, deepening and limiting all natural love, so that our love, awakened by the Word, should take on the quality of a witness to that Word.
