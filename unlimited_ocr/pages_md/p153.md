<|det|>text [58, 110, 876, 379]<|/det|>Now there are other Christians who think that this outlook stands in irreconcilable opposition to the Christian faith and who deny that those who hold it can be called Christians at all. And there are others again who find the contradiction oppressive which exists between their scientific convictions and the principles which guide them in their daily lives, on the one hand, and on the other the miracle stories of the New Testament; who would by all means like to adhere to the Christian faith, but who suppose a Christian is obliged to consider these stories to be true. Since they cannot do that, they fall into doubt as to whether they may still legitimately claim to be Christians in any sense.
<|det|>text [58, 381, 876, 603]<|/det|>What are we to say to all this? In the first place, that belief in the miracle stories of the New Testament is not in fact the essence of Christian faith. Christian faith means rather faith in the grace of God which has been manifested in Christ. As Luther has already said, the real work of Christ consists in His victory over the law and over death. And to believe in Christ means to believe in Him as the One who frees us from the tyranny of the law and from the reign of death; but it does not mean considering the miracle stories of the New Testament to be true.
<|det|>text [58, 605, 876, 879]<|/det|>And what are we to say to those who retort: No! Belief in the miracles of the New Testament is also an integral part of the Christian faith. It is just in such assent to the truth of these stories that Christian faith is evidenced. For Christian faith means surrendering not only one's self-will but also the whole domain of one's thinking; it means not setting up one's own thoughts against the authority of God's word. If in the life of faith we are called to sacrifice to God all that we are and have, we must also sacrifice to Him our life on the intellectual plane. And if these miraculous happenings are impossible according to the mode of our present-day thinking, then the latter is simply false and must be given up.
