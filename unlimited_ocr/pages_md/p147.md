<|det|>text [67, 201, 885, 719]<|/det|>And have we not to some extent already experienced how this call of God, extremely inconvenient and disturbing as it was, nevertheless became a blessing for us? Have we not known how, just by being torn out of a life of custom and routine, we have been preserved from inner torpidity and indolence? That our life thus received a new impulse and a new depth (whether through our own pain or through our perception of another's sorrow) so that then for the first time we realized what it meant to live? Have we not felt in such circumstances that we enjoy more deeply and gratefully the happiness that was given us to enjoy, and that we found a new and closer link with our families? That in days of dark distress we became more patient, and from hard times were permitted to reap a harvest of blessing? How easy it is for us to become buried in the sameness of the days; how easily we are deadened by the routine of life, so that our personal life and its spiritual condition grows to be a matter of indifference to us! Or again, how easily we get agitated by things which are not worth such excitement! God's call, extremely disturbing and inconvenient as it is, shakes us up and causes us to throw off our lethargy, restoring to us our true life and enabling us once more to view things in their true perspective.
