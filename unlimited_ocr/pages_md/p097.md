<|det|>text [78, 83, 893, 398]<|/det|>In the time of John the Baptist and Jesus the question was a very living one and the hearts of the Jewish people were aglow with hope in the One who was to come. We refer to the nation's hope in the Messiah, the King who was to reign in the latter days of blessedness, who was to bring to an end all sorrow and wrong on the earth and to set up the kingdom of God, a kingdom of righteousness and salvation. Such hopes arose from the words of the ancient prophets, which were carefully preserved in the Scriptures and cherished with yearning hearts. And from hopes of this kind arose the question: When will the expected Messiah come? Who will it be? “Watchman, what of the night?” And it is from the ferment of such hopes that Jesus is challenged with the question: “Are you He?”
<|det|>text [78, 400, 893, 690]<|/det|>The question flows from the Messianic hope. Hence we ourselves can ask such a question only if such a hope is alive in us. It is a question which has no meaning for all those who are satisfied with the world as it is. Whether it be that the world is for them a place of pleasure and enjoyment, where they disregard the shadows which of course there are in life, and cling to the light that is given them, banishing cares from their minds and seizing the joys which each day brings. Or again it might be that the world for such people is a sphere in which they fulfil a vocation to work and struggle, accomplishing their tasks either as individuals or communities, tasks which reward initiative and self-sacrifice, and make life worth living.
