When we have this faith, then God is present for us not only in nature but in the world and life as a whole, in history as a whole with its heights and depths, in the humdrum world of every day as well as in the relaxation of festival days. The consequence is that the presence of God in nature takes on for us a new meaning. What is that?
<|det|>text [58, 246, 885, 587]<|/det|>1. He who has this faith understands the presence of God in nature as the invitation of the God who is calling man unto Himself, who is driving man to seek Himself. In this light he understands too that nature-piety of which we have been speaking. He understands it as a flight to God, which is not aware of its own true nature and meaning. For if nature-piety is genuine and true, it is a witness to the sin which is the controlling factor in human history and the life of responsibility and anxiety. As an escape from the humdrum world of everyday life and work, it bears witness to the fact that this life is empty of God, that God is not felt to be expressed within it. But precisely as such a flight, nature-piety attests also that man is driven on every side to seek God and is impelled by the yearning to attain the presence of God, which embraces man with sustaining and liberating power.
<|det|>text [58, 587, 885, 791]<|/det|>2. The Christian believer rejoices in the presence of God in nature; the word which calls him to believe causes him also to be certain of the nearness of God in nature, and to receive with thankfulness and praise all the manifold gifts of nature as the gifts of God. And again, the divine in nature constantly leads him back to the divine presence which speaks out in the Word; for he does not defy nature, he sees that in spite of all her gifts she remains ambiguous for man; it is the hidden God who is present in her.
