It spells release for man not only when he feels his anxieties dissipated but also when the pressure of responsibility slackens. He feels emancipated when control is taken out of his own hands and he knows himself to be sustained by a mightier hand, when he no longer needs to give his life its law, but knows himself to be subject to a greater law, which relieves him of ultimate care and responsibility, when he becomes aware that his life too is governed by the divine ordinance: “While the earth remains . . .” And it is just this feeling of release through being controlled and enclasped by an infinite power, which man experiences—even if only momentarily—when he opens his heart to the subduing beauty of nature. For the infinite power which is here perceptible to him in sensuous embodiment—can he call it anything else but God? And further, it is no accident that the human imagination, when it delights to picture to itself the bliss which surrounds the Deity, represents paradise as a gloriously beautiful garden.
