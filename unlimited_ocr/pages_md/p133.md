It is the danger that in spite of all our faithful devotion to our duties, our personal selves become dissipated through the pressure of our cares and anxieties, that our being is consumed away and sucked dry by the obsession of our work. Or again we might lose our souls through those distractions in which we relax from our work. We in fact run this risk even when we appreciate and foster spiritual values such as enrich our lives, for example the world of truth and beauty. May not all this be something which, in spite of the interest and enjoyment it brings, in the last resort signifies nothing decisive for our inner life?
<|det|>text [58, 291, 881, 742]<|/det|>But we are to be something of value in ourselves; from all that engages our attention and interest, from duty and work, from care and sorrow, from our relaxations and pleasures, we must return to ourselves and collect and concentrate our inner spiritual life. Are we something of value intrinsically? May not this question have occurred to us at times when we lay sick in bed? There are many men who, when sickness or old age makes them incapable of work, feel that they are faced as it were with emptiness, and begin to wonder for what purpose they exist at all. Have such people really lived or have they lost their true life in preoccupation with their work and business? Do we imagine that the real worth of a man lies in his actual performance, in what he has produced considered apart from what he is in himself, and not in what he is as a human being, not in the love which streams from him to brighten our lives? As though a life which is quite incapable of producing anything and which from the point of view of objective production is a burden to the community, might not in truth be a secret source of blessing to the community!
<|det|>text [58, 743, 881, 901]<|/det|>Or perhaps the inner void of our lives has become painfully apparent to us at some moment when we have been called upon to help and comfort another whose life was shadowed by inner personal troubles and temptations? How can we help others when we are nothing in ourselves? Only the man who is intrinsically something of value is capable of fellowship with others, of friendship and love.
