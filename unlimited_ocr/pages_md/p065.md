Inasmuch as it closes its mind to the challenge of the word of Jesus and fails to grasp that its own existence is being questioned, it remains abandoned to itself and lost; because it shuts out the divine light it remains in darkness, however little it realizes this and however much it supposes it has the light. But it is also judged in that its power is broken. The power of the world is not destroyed by the fact that there are courageous men of steadfast character who are ready to sacrifice themselves for Jesus. It is not we who execute the judgment on the world by resisting it.

<|det|>text [72, 413, 897, 750]<|/det|>Rather the power of the world is broken as a result of the fact that the word of Jesus is proclaimed in it and again and again endows weak men with the power to resist the world. Probably the most subtle seduction of the world lies in the fact that, in so far as it can respect human heroism, it also respects brave Christians. And of course the Christian by his courage must secure respect for the cause he represents. But for the cause itself! It is not a question of his own human qualities being respected. And the Christian knows that even the most ardent readiness for sacrifice does not in itself prove the rightness of his cause. Were it not so, then it would depend on the Christian himself to achieve victory over the prince of this world. But the Scripture says: "Of judgment because the ruler of this world is judged." He is already judged and his power already broken.
