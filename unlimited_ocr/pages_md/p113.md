Do we not normally become once more quickly caught up in life with its claims, its this-worldly values and purposes, the pressing responsibilities of the day and moment? So that, in fine, this world is for us equated with reality? But is it not possible that we viewed reality more deeply and fully in those moments of failure in which all the turmoil and striving of this world struck us as alien and, in essence, of questionable value?

<|det|>text [52, 342, 870, 789]<|/det|>Yes, in fact it should always be so; we should always be prepared to see the realities of this world and our life within them as appearance only, in which genuine reality does not inhere—or which can become real only through ourselves, when we ourselves are living in the power of a higher, more genuine reality as a result of which we can transform our life in the world. For of course the Christian faith and discipleship to Jesus is not a mere matter of resignation, it is not merely a feeling of homelessness in the world, it is not simply something negative. Rather it is just the consciousness of living in the fullness of a power which streams from supernatural, ultimate reality. But to receive this power demands the readiness to adopt an attitude of inner detachment from the world, so that we move in it as though we did not belong to it, are active within it, rejoice or mourn in it as though we did not do so. In the last resort this means that we should be detached from ourselves, from that restless life which we know to be active in our wishes and plans, our work and our enjoyment. What is required from us is the confession that we are not those who see, but are blind.
