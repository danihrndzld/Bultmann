<|det|>text [106, 193, 932, 508]<|/det|>To be a Christian means to be one who waits for God's future. Hence for the Christian perhaps all seasons are essentially an Advent season. For Advent is characterized above all by this note of expectation. Yes, so it is, and yet it seems good to distinguish the season of Advent in the round of the Church's year as the special time of expectation. It is intended to remind us sharply of what we so easily and so often forget, namely that, as Christians, we are expectant. But also it should make us more certain of the fulfilment of the promise, for it leads us on to that feast which is the very symbol of fulfilment, the Feast of the Holy Nativity. Is it merely a symbol of fulfilment? Or is it also already fulfilment itself, in so far as fulfilment is possible in the context of our earthly pilgrimage?
<|det|>text [106, 509, 932, 820]<|/det|>Yes, it is certainly fulfilment provided we celebrate the feast in its true sense, by opening our hearts to the message of the divine love which is rooted in the birth of Jesus Christ. It depends on us, whether Christmas will signify for us a real fulfilment. The more difficult it may seem to us this year to keep Christmas with the joy normally associated with it, so much the more should we remind ourselves that the true Christian joy is promised to those who wait upon God. And so all the heaviness which lies on our hearts may serve to make us realize that we are the expectant ones who trust in God. Thus we shall be prepared to receive the true Christmas joy which will transform our human expectancy into a trustful, glad and consoled waiting for the gift of God's future.
