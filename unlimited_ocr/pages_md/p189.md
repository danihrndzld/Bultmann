<|det|>text [63, 119, 888, 365]<|/det|>Hence we may perhaps easily understand, in the light of such human experience, the freedom implied by “that day” which ultimately frees us from all those things with which this world binds and burdens us. Does there not steal over us at times a longing for such freedom? A longing to be cleansed from all that stains and clouds our being? A longing for a unity of being through deliverance from all that confuses and divides our spirit? And that longing which sometimes we feel for the vanished past of our childhood, with its artless delights, is it not a longing for freedom from the confusions of this world?
<|det|>text [65, 368, 892, 706]<|/det|>But do we seriously desire such freedom? We must confess: this freedom is in its roots freedom from ourselves. For all with which the world exercises its hold over us, good and ill, pleasure and pain, gains its binding power from ourselves; from the fact that we have our wishes, our sick desires, our projects and plans, which the world sometimes meets with alluring hopes of fulfilment, sometimes crowns with a measure of success, sometimes severely suppresses and destroys. It is from the well of our own being that vexing questions and anxieties arise, because it is we who generate desires and structures of life. If “that day” is ever to come for us, the day on which we shall ask no further questions, then it must come as the day on which our self-will, the motivating force of our wishes and plans, grows silent; as the day on which the world for us sinks into nothingness.
