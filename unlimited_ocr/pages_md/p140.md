<|det|>text [108, 119, 931, 345]<|/det|>In order rightly to understand the story which Jesus narrates in our text, we must remember a custom which existed in the country and at the time of Jesus. When a man of means proposed to give a grand banquet, he first issued a preliminary invitation, without specifying the day and the hour of its taking place. Thus the guests were warned that they should hold themselves in readiness for an occasion in the near future. Then, when the day came, a second invitation was issued: “The day is here; come, for everything is prepared!”
<|det|>text [108, 346, 931, 795]<|/det|>In accordance with this custom, the rich man of whom our story tells had invited many guests and of course they had accepted his invitation; for who would want to miss such a banquet? But they had accepted somewhat casually; for, as we see, when the time came they were not ready. They did not organize their time and business with a view to the fact that shortly the host's messenger would come and invite them for the particular day, but they went on with their affairs and daily pursuits as though the final invitation to the feast were not shortly to be expected; in fact they probably forgot the invitation altogether. When therefore the messenger comes, the attention of all of them is claimed by things which for the moment seem more important than the banquet. One has bought a field and must go out to consider how to exploit it best; another has bought oxen and must go and look after them; the third has married a wife and therefore is prevented. The remainder who are not expressly mentioned have one thing or another which precludes their coming. In short, none of those who were bidden have now time to come; none comes to the banquet.
