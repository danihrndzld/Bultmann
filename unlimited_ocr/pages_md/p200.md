This is also the meaning of the second beatitude: “Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted.” Yes, this is the message which we are to-day most eager to relate to our own position. But again it is not sorrow as such to which God’s message of healing and deliverance applies; it is a question of understanding rightly the meaning of this word. All sorrow and pain is intended to make us aware how insecure is the ground on which we stand; in every individual case of distress and pain there is but manifested the deep underlying world agony which envelops humanity as a whole. In every loss that we suffer we see the evidence of that power making for transience, the power of death to which we are all subject. Do we understand our sorrows in this light? For the most part they strike us as something strange and offensive, astonishing and frightening. We say, bewildered, “No, that cannot be!” and fail to recognize that it is but the common human lot. In spite of all that is beautiful and uplifting, human life, as the ancient poet Pindar says, is but a shadowy dream. We go our way as over a surface of ice, which at any moment may break.
