<|det|>text [69, 100, 892, 460]<|/det|>Just as arrogance which insists on its rights corrupts the life both of the individual and the community, so also it is arrogance which in times of sore distress arouses a spirit of complaint and accusation, and prevents sorrow from bearing the fruits of grace. No doubt there are haughty and defiant men who are steeled by suffering. But are they really matured thereby? There is a defiant harshness of character which chooses self-isolation and reserve, and which conceals an inner cold-heartedness and spiritual void. It may well be that such a man is invulnerable to the blows of fate; but he is also blind to the graciousness of fate. There is also a character which is steeled by heavy sorrow and which is truly matured and strengthened thereby, a character of cheerful open-heartedness, of inner spiritual wealth; a character which gives itself freely to all, in warmth of heart which radiates itself on all with whom it comes into contact.
<|det|>text [69, 463, 892, 891]<|/det|>This is genuine power, whereas cold harshness is only apparent power and behind its reserve hides an inner weakness. Genuine strength of character does not fortify itself against fate in an attitude of proud reserve, but exposes itself to fate in the confidence that whatever fate brings can only make it grow stronger and richer. Such a man understands and accepts his destiny as a real source of grace and blessing. Moreover he has the inner freedom of spirit to enjoy the beautiful and the good which do come to him, the sunny days through which it is granted him to pass, in a spirit of serenity, without fear and without a bad conscience. For were we not resigned to whatever destiny may bring, how could we with a good conscience enjoy the good which is granted us, when all around us there is so much distress and misery?
