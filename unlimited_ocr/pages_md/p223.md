"Whoever is wise, accepts God's gifts with patience and joy; but the highest insight is needed if our wisdom is not to focus itself on what lies exposed to our view (in that case we should have to despair), but on what is future, unknown and not exposed to our gaze" (II, 206, 27ff.). Hence this is the choice and decision which lie before us. But whoever can say: "The Lord is my portion", can also continue: "The Lord is good to those who wait for Him, to the soul that seeks Him" (3: 25).
<|det|>text [79, 346, 904, 569]<|/det|>Oh that we might succeed at one stroke in deciding aright this either-or! Then at one stroke the burden of our anxieties would drop from us. Free and unencumbered could we traverse our trials and distresses, inwardly freed from self-will, and no evil could any longer affect us in our inmost self. There may well be people who succeed in doing so, who in a childlike spirit of confidence pass calmly through all terrors, as in the fairy story the lucky child passes cheerful and unharmed among roaming wild beasts which can do him no hurt, because he is magically protected.
<|det|>text [74, 570, 900, 886]<|/det|>But how few there are whose childlike faith as a magic armour protects them from all the assaults of the devil, from doubt and bitter anxiety and fear! We others, who are by far the greater number—must we not ever strive with might and main to secure this magic armour and to appropriate this faith? Must we not repeatedly renew the decision, and win an ever new victory over ourselves as we determine that the Lord shall be our portion? To us is addressed with special force the verse: “It is good that one should wait quietly for the salvation of the Lord” (3: 26). “Wait quietly”—that is what we must learn to do. Such patience implies that we shall ever renew our decision, confess our faith in the Lord, turn away from ourselves and from the world, and hope in “the salvation of the Lord”.
