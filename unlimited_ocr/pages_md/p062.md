<|det|>text [100, 116, 928, 319]<|/det|>But in what sense is it meant that a true judgment takes place, to which the Spirit opens the eyes of the Christian? The Spirit will convict the world of “sin and righteousness and judgment”; i.e. the Spirit will show what these three words in truth mean, and, at the same time, who is the sinner, who the righteous man, and who is judged. The world also is of course familiar with these three words; it speaks about sin and justice and judgment and supposes it knows what they mean.
<|det|>text [100, 320, 928, 634]<|/det|>The world understands by sin, moral failings and guilty deeds. It does not hesitate to condemn sin; nevertheless it does not take sin seriously. It thinks of sin as of something occasional, fortuitous, a deviation which might possibly occur. It does not realize that if anyone sins he is the slave of sin (John 8: 34). And if anyone suggests that the world is sinful, it derides him or is angry. For it is of the very essence of the self-consciousness and need of the world, that it should consider itself to be right and good. It holds the question of sin to be a question which it has to decide and settle from within its own sphere, before its own forum and according to its own law. And it does not realize that the word “sin” presupposes a reference to a judge beyond the world; it is unfamiliar with the concept of God’s consuming holiness.
