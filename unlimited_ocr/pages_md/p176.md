In order to illustrate the character of God's action, Jesus narrates this story of the householder and the labourers in the vineyard. In any event the householder behaves quite correctly in so far as he faithfully gives to his labourers the agreed payment at the due time. When those who were first hired complain that they do not receive more, he is quite justified in his reply: "Friend, I am doing you no wrong: did you not agree with me for a denarius?" What proper answer can the grumbling workman give? It is quite evident that the householder is right; the labourer can raise no just claim.
<|det|>text [96, 348, 927, 706]<|/det|>And that is the first thing we are meant to learn from this parable: man can raise no just claim in the sight of God. God gives us what is our due, and no one has the right to complain and require more. But the disposition to think that we can make claims on God clings to us men and so oftentimes we are accustomed to look at others who are faring better than ourselves, and to complain: by what means have they deserved better than we? Are there not many of these lucky people who have toiled far less than ourselves? who have endured far less trial and vexation? Yet they are to fare better than we! With what justification? May we not claim as much? Or else we moan: why am I in particular struck by evil which is spared others? Yet they are no better than I! In fact it is just those whose conduct is less exemplary who so often escape! It is not seldom that thoughts and feelings of this kind sway men's minds.
