<|det|>text [60, 292, 889, 588]<|/det|>THE words of our Lord to which we have just been listening are among the most familiar in the whole of the New Testament. They bring vividly before us the figure of the Lord Jesus Christ who extends His arms invitingly and appeals to men: "Come to me!" The call of Jesus sounds in our life in many various ways. Let us think to-day of the way in which that appeal comes to us through the festival that we are celebrating, namely Sunday. To-day is Sunday, a day which the ancient Church described as the Lord's Day. On this, the Lord's Day, the bells make the voice of Jesus resound throughout the Christian world with the appeal: "Come to me!" What has Sunday to say to us, this day on which the appeal of Christ strikes home to us with peculiar power?

<|det|>text [58, 653, 888, 903]<|/det|>The Christian Sunday is foreshadowed in the Jewish Sabbath. The latter was celebrated at the end of the week, Sunday at the beginning. This characteristic fact in itself suggests that Sunday should spell for us a new beginning, a new beginning after the week of toil and the cares of everyday life. What a blessing it is for man that again and again he is allowed to make such a new beginning! How many a time can a new day be a real gift to us!
