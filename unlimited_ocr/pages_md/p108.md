<|det|>text [108, 356, 940, 581]<|/det|>This year we are observing the season of Advent in time of war, and are preparing for a wartime Christmas. Whether there is war or not, nature follows its fixed regular pattern of seasons. After the bitter cold of winter the springtime sun will shine; the earth will grow warm, green leaves and flowers will burst into bloom, and the human heart, even though it is wartime, will be cheered and animated by this renewal of nature. Is that the case also with regard to Christmas? Will it come and bring its gift to us with the same certainty as ever?
<|det|>text [108, 583, 940, 899]<|/det|>Yes and no! To be sure, the Christmas message will be proclaimed as usual when the day comes; and the immemorial customs bound up with its celebration will as ever somehow lend a festal glow to our houses. But does that signify the real coming of Christmas, which comes of course only when its message penetrates the individual heart? Certainly the message of the angel is meant for all: "Behold, I bring you good news of a great joy." But everything depends on whether the individual yields himself to this joy. Whether Christmas really comes to us means after all whether joy really floods our hearts in spite of all our care, anxiety and distress. The true coming of Christmas into our lives is essentially a question of the coming of the light of life to drive away our darkness.
