Hence the Christian does not allow his life to be determined by a self-chosen aim, to which all his energies and hopes are bent; but his life is rather characterized, in a certain sense, by lack of specific aim, by which we mean an inner freedom from self-chosen aims. The faith of the Christian is that the future will bring him his true self, which he can never capture by his own self-appointed courses. In other words, readiness for my fate, for that which God designs to do with me.

<|det|>text [92, 494, 926, 903]<|/det|>Most men live, unconsciously to themselves, in a state of characteristic inner discord. Their hopes for the future without which they cannot live are combined with a fear of the future which can rob them of what they already possess. In so far as they strive to hold fast to what they already possess, they are clinging desperately to the past and not living towards the future but dying to it. What they hope for from the future is always only something which, once they have it, they will be eager to grasp and retain, so as to possess it, and secure themselves against the future. It is from such strivings of humanity, from such eagerness to hold fast and possess, that there arises the curse which weighs on the whole of human history; the strife of all against all, because each wishes to assert himself and his rights and to secure his future against others. And instead of living accessibly to the future, men really live on the basis of the past, which prescribes to them their aims, dictates their motives and the criteria of their conduct.
