<|det|>text [65, 29, 885, 235]<|/det|>religious feelings of gratitude which man experiences in response to the beauty of nature are really awakened by the presence of God and are directed towards Him. For nature is His creation, and if with sensitive and true hearts we honour the beauty of creation, we are in fact honouring God the Creator. And yet if our devout thankfulness stops at nature herself and does not go beyond her to the vision of the Creator, then our religious response to nature is no more than idolatry.
<|det|>text [65, 236, 885, 555]<|/det|>What is the real nature of the wrong which inheres in such an attitude? It lies in the fact that by this attitude the majesty of God is violated. God dwells in unapproachable light (1 Tim. 6: 16), and if He is present in nature He is also concealed in nature. He does not allow Himself to be directly seen by man—we can only believe in Him; and all the splendours of nature and her teeming energies are not God Himself, they do but point to God who, Himself unseen, wraps Himself in the garb of nature. But the error of man is this, that he wishes to look upon God and to hold un-mediated communion with Him. And thus for him creation becomes the Creator, the time of the world is equated with eternity, and he forgets the significance of the word “While the earth remains”, and the limit it implies.
<|det|>text [65, 557, 885, 899]<|/det|>The fruits of this error are soon seen in the life of men, for every wrong is avenged. We do not propose to dwell on the way in which the error becomes most plainly visible. For in fact man, who perhaps at first fostered genuinely religious feelings of delight in nature, soon came to think that the energies of nature whose inexhaustible power and abundance he admires are divine, and when he remembers that his own human being has its natural side—birth and growth, eating and drinking, waking and sleeping—he easily succumbs to the delusion that he too, man, is also a divine being. He feels that a divine energy and a sacred will are expressed in his blood, and in the impulses to which it gives rise.
