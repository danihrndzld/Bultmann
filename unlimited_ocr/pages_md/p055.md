<|det|>text [33, 90, 866, 631]<|/det|>Of course at this moment of the story, when events are breaking over them, the disciples are not in a position to understand the inner meaning of those events. “I have yet many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now.” They have understood only one thing, namely that their Master is about to depart from them and leave them bereft; and their heart is filled with sadness. Their gaze is focused on what is visibly happening around them with a tangible impact on themselves; and therefore they are bewildered and in despair. The new insight which the Spirit of truth will bestow on them consists in this, that their eyes will be opened to the invisible aspect of events and to what constitutes the real and true happening of the present hour. This is the truth into which the Spirit is to lead them; the reality behind the visible, which alone gives to all that is visible its essential significance. The Spirit opens our eyes to the invisible working of God, which is veiled by the visible events taking place in the world. For the ultimately real and true is not what intrudes itself on our notice, what sets in motion our feeling, thinking and willing, what stuns us with its terror or perhaps may thrill us with its magnificence. No; only behind all that lies the fundamentally true and real, namely that which God is effecting, and which therefore alone has true meaning for our lives.
<|det|>text [38, 633, 870, 835]<|/det|>Once the Spirit has opened our eyes to this inner truth and reality, then we shall no longer consider the visible and apparent as the fundamental but shall measure its significance and value in the light of what it means to God, and we shall be able to penetrate to its innocence or falsehood. For its significance might be much greater but also much more trivial than we first supposed; it may be more pleasing but also more terrifying than we surmised in the first instance.
