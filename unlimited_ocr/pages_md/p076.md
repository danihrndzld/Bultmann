<|det|>text [108, 201, 928, 267]<|/det|>There is only one power which can free us from the bondage of self, and take away from us both our fear and our self-despair. It is the power of love.
<|det|>text [108, 268, 930, 718]<|/det|>Through our human intercourse and dealings with each other, whenever our common life is struck by the beams of divine love, we know what the spirit and sway of love can mean. And some of us are aware with shame and gratitude that we are not what we have made of ourselves but what the love that we have encountered, and that has been bestowed on us, has enabled us to become. This mysterious power and grace of genuine love rests upon this fact, namely that it sees and understands and deals with a human being whom it encounters, not as what he in effect at present is, but as what he can and should and would like to be, as the being whom he will be. It cheers and enheartens us when we perceive that others are treating us as those whom we shall be; it is an inspiration to us to see that the other can penetrate beyond all that is outward, beyond the grime of daily life, the petty and trivial, the imperfect, the half-hearted that adheres to each one of us; that the person with whom we are dealing trusts us. And to trust a man means of course to believe in his future. Such trust inspires the one who experiences it with new happiness and sense of freedom.
<|det|>text [108, 720, 930, 900]<|/det|>Thus love bestows a real creative future and at the same time belief in its unbounded horizons. But all our love is only a faint reflection of the love of God, which “moves sun and stars”. How feeble and wavering is our power to give and awaken love! Yet the essential message of Christianity is that the love of God has been manifested in Jesus Christ.
