<|det|>text [48, 40, 874, 515]<|/det|>The tax collector has nothing of all this. He looks not on others but on himself alone: “God be merciful to me a sinner!” He knows and confesses that in the presence of God he can raise no claim and must cast himself utterly on the divine mercy. He would like to enjoy some favour with God: “God be merciful to me a sinner!” But he has completely surrendered that claimfulness which supposes that by our own achievements we can acquire merit with God. It is just this which is the fundamental sin: to presume by one’s own strength to assert a claim before God. In the presence of God no man can be worth anything on the ground of his own merit; none can boast before God. Such boasting infringes the honour of God as Creator. “What have you that you did not receive? If then you received it, why do you boast as if it were not a gift?” (1 Cor. 4: 7.) In his claimfulness man forgets that he is God’s creature. And from this point of view it is a matter of indifference whether he speaks of God as does the Pharisee or whether he ceases to speak of God. Both attitudes are sinful; both show man’s self-assertive claims in the face of God; this is sin and self-deception.
