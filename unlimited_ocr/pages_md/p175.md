<|det|>text [73, 238, 895, 710]<|/det|>This impression is strengthened if we set beside the parable of Jesus that of a Jewish scribe which outwardly bears a complete resemblance to it. This parable speaks of a king who had hired many workers for the completion of a task. One of them distinguished himself by his industry and skill, with the result that the king took him by the hand and walked to and fro with him. At evening time the workers came and the king gave them all the same reward. Those who had worked the whole day long complained saying: "We have worked all day long and this fellow has worked for only two hours, yet he also has received full payment!" The king made answer: "In two hours he achieved more than you did in the whole day!" Thus in this instance too the same reward was given for an unequal amount of work, yet this was only so in appearance. In reality an equal amount of work was performed. Our sense of justice is thus satisfied. For in this case it is made clear why the shorter time of work was rewarded equally with the longer time; in the shorter interval as much was performed as in the longer. Here too then the principle is observed: equal pay for equal performance!
