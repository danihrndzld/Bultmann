<|det|>text [79, 35, 892, 80]<|/det|>His life is to a certain extent like the running of a race, in which he wishes to attain his own true being.
<|det|>text [79, 81, 897, 282]<|/det|>But this is also true of him who is not striving—or not essentially striving—for any such particular goal, but who sees the meaning of life in the education of himself, in the formation of his own inner culture, in the perfecting of himself and his capacities; who wishes to make something of himself, who works at himself in order to be able to stand the test of his own self-examination. “Before each of us there hovers an ideal of what we must become, and so long as we have not reached it, our hearts are restless.”
<|det|>text [79, 284, 897, 709]<|/det|>Such a one too lives in the strength of the future, of what he is not yet. He himself knows that he has not yet reached his goal. And the more frankly he admits this to himself, the more he becomes aware how often he has gone and goes astray, how often he has lost himself instead of finding himself, how often he has been guilty of falling short of his ideals. And just as little as he would care to stand exposed to the eyes of all in his present state, so much the more, in all his restlessness, is he comforted by the thought that his true being is not what he here and now is, but rather what he hopes one day to become. Hence no doubt it is generally true that all men live by the inspiration of the future, whether they are clearly aware of this or not. But it makes a decisive difference whether the future which animates and fashions the life of a man is a real and genuine future, i.e. whether it is only dreamed of, longed for, striven for, or whether it is in fact coming to me. For after all, the future is what is coming to us whether we hope for it, expect, and are prepared for it, or not.
<|det|>text [79, 711, 897, 891]<|/det|>The extent to which men are living in view of the real future is shown simply by this, namely, how far they are able to deal with what the future brings to them, or whether on the contrary they collapse and are crushed under it, be it joy or pain or the dreary round of humdrum duties.
