And this means that in reality he does not know God, the Almighty and All-present, at all. For to know this God means, of course, to experience His liberating and sustaining power and His controlling guidance not only when we step forth out of the hum of daily life, in infrequent, alas! too infrequent pauses, which stand out as islands of repose in the roaring torrent of everyday affairs; but to have the assurance of His liberating and sustaining power and to feel the security of His guiding hand in life as a whole, with its care and responsibility, its sorrow and its guilt. And if already in our surrender to nature we experience a sense of liberation through which our cares vanish and anxieties become silent, it is that nature gives us an intimation of God who bestows upon us that freedom which enables us in the midst of the cares of life to live without fretful care and in the responsibilities of life to feel the burden fall from our shoulders, since He cares for us and stands forth as the One who is ultimately responsible for us.

<|det|>text [94, 544, 929, 891]<|/det|>But such faith is the Christian faith which as such is awakened by the message of the grace of God in Jesus Christ. Now this message is a message of forgiveness—a forgiveness which does not simply mean that God forgives us this or that moral failing, as among ourselves we can and should forgive each other our lapses. It declares rather that God forgives and has forgiven the fundamental sin of our whole existence, the one essential sin which is that we have separated ourselves from God and have as it were cut ourselves off from Him in order to live independently in our own strength. In Christ He has created a new humanity; the humanity of those who allow Christ the Good Shepherd to lead them back to God, the Omnipotent and Omnipresent, from whom, to whom, and through whom, are all things.
