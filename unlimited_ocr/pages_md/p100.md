<|det|>text [92, 48, 923, 273]<|/det|>This hope only faded with the period when man began to suppose that by knowledge and technical science he could wholly subjugate the world to his own dominion. We are now living in this age, and indeed we are at its climax since we not only understand how to organize and exploit the powers of nature by technical mastery, but also suppose that we can control the life of man by means of an ingeniously contrived and subtly elaborated technique designed to ensure complete dominance over the minds of men. Thus it is hoped to establish an empire of success.
<|det|>text [96, 274, 921, 657]<|/det|>How in such circumstances shall our vision penetrate beyond this visible world into the realm of the eternal and invisible? How should we lift up our eyes to the hills whence comes our help, since we expect all needed help to flow from our own insight and power of accomplishment? How should we still hope for the end of this world and its ways? How should we still be able to ask the question concerning the One who is to come? But this is the mind of Christians, that they suffer in face of the world and its woes. Not that they withdraw themselves from the world, or refuse gratefully to receive the world's gifts, or to co-operate in the work of the world with a full sense of responsibility. But it is well that they realize that all this world's gifts and demands cannot make a man happy within, nor can they in the last resort claim the surrender of his being. Christians know that in this world there can be no reign of peace and justice and pure joy.
<|det|>text [92, 658, 925, 907]<|/det|>How do they know this? They cannot hide from themselves the sway which death exercises over the earth, nor transfigure it with fine-sounding words.
