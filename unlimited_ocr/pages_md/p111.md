<|det|>text [53, 336, 883, 722]<|/det|>This “light of life” Jesus promises to them who follow Him. The price to be paid for the gift is that of discipleship. What does this mean? At bottom it means nothing other than truly to desire what is promised. The peculiar feature here is that the word of promise is the word of judgment also: “For judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see and that those who see may become blind.” It is the very word of promise which is at the same time a word declaratory of judgment; the gift itself, the “light of life” effects judgment. For it effects a discrimination between those who accept the gift and those who close their hearts against it. The judgment that is here in question is not a special arrangement for the end of time, but a process consummated in the hearing of the gospel proclamation itself. Elsewhere we read, “And this is the judgment, that the light has come into the world and men loved darkness rather than light” (John 3: 19).
