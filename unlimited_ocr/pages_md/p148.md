<|det|>text [106, 339, 930, 519]<|/det|>This brings us to the main point. To be ready for God's call means just this—to be prepared to live in the conviction that our life of earthly work with its cares and projects, its sorrows and joys, is not the ultimate reality. What then is the essence of God's invitation? It is surely the call to a higher life, to a future life, to a life that lies beyond this world. It is the call to free ourselves from this world and to become centred in God's world.
<|det|>text [106, 521, 930, 905]<|/det|>No doubt when things are going badly with us here, when we feel acutely the oppressiveness of our wretched and distressing fate, we long to soar beyond it all and we eagerly listen to this call which invites us to another world where there is no more pain and sorrow. But God's call demands more than this. It demands that in good as in bad times we should be deeply aware that this our world can give us no final satisfaction, can bestow on us no true fullness of life. God's call faces us with the decision between our world and His world, between the present and the future, between the here and now and the beyond. Do we really want the reality of God or do we in fact want our world and God only occasionally for the purpose of transfiguring and enhancing our world? Is it the fact that as far as we are concerned, what we plan and do here, the structures of life which we carve out and build with all the trouble and care involved, what causes us sorrow or joy, is the supreme and final thing?
