<|det|>text [65, 226, 882, 472]<|/det|>What then is the meaning of miracle from the standpoint of the Christian religion? To what extent is Christian belief a belief in miracles? At all events, miracle implies an action wrought by God. To believe in miracle means, as we have already suggested, to be prepared to believe that the miraculous working of God meets us in our lives. It has often been said that all events are essentially miraculous. For if we believe in God as the Omnipotent, the Creator of heaven and earth, we must also be persuaded that all things are traceable to His action and energy and that all that happens to us is miraculous.
<|det|>text [60, 476, 880, 791]<|/det|>It is no doubt true that belief in God as Creator implies that all events are ultimately the effect of His operation. However, the situation is that the creative activity of God remains hidden from us, that we do not always and everywhere recognize and experience Him as Creator. Normally we view life and the world as an ordered state of things and as a process of controlled happenings grounded in the law of cause and effect. What takes place here springs partly from the movement of nature which is regulated by laws that we know, and whose forces we are more and more able to bring within our control, and partly from the impulsion of the human will and the planning of the human mind—things which are also intelligible to us. Where then does the miraculous creative action of God come into play?
