"For all that the Lord created is good and nothing is bad which s received with a grateful heart." Only the pleasure in the enjoyment of which we could not thank the Lord as Giver, nor indeed think of Him at all, but would rather have to avoid all religious thoughts, is "worldly pleasure" which we ought to shun not only on Sunday but on weekdays also. And even other pleasures which we might thankfully welcome as His gift become "worldly pleasures", if in enjoying them we forget Him and the thanks due to Him. "Whether then you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God." It is this which we are to learn from the Lord; this is the meaning of His yoke, and this should stand as the guiding and testing rule over all that we do on the Lord's Day.

<|det|>text [52, 495, 874, 608]<|/det|>And finally, one last word! Sunday should mean for us a new beginning not merely in regard to our week-day work. It is not simply the humdrum work of the week which causes us to feel the blessing of a new beginning Sunday by Sunday.
<|det|>text [52, 609, 874, 903]<|/det|>"A new beginning"—how often is that the wish of our hearts when we look back on our past with all its working days and Sundays! For how often do we realize with a painful sense of oppression that we are caught in and enchained by our past, that we have lost our way in life and can find no means of escape from our difficulties. We have enslaved ourselves to one thing or another—obligations, customs, passions! We note that as a result we have become fettered and dishonoured, that we are perishing, and cannot get free from the toils. At such moments we are aware how weary and heavy laden we in fact are, not only through work and care but also—and still more oppressively—through guilt and impurity. Oh that we might begin all over again!
