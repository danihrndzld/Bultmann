It is right to refuse to feel dependent on the calculations of the other party; but it is not right to resist the sense of dependence on his kindness.
<|det|>text [106, 158, 932, 606]<|/det|>True giving binds two people together in perfect freedom without implying any material obligation of the one party towards the other. The acceptance of gifts of this kind requires open-heartedness, glad and spontaneous submission to the kindness of the giver. And it is just this attitude which so many people cannot bring themselves to adopt. They suppose that it means yielding ground too much and they shut themselves up in an attitude of hard inaccessibility, from the sheer need to assert their power and authority. They do not know the happiness that lies precisely in receiving gifts and thereby winning favour. This is especially clear from the fact that so many people resist receiving from others one of the greatest of gifts which a man can bestow on his neighbour: namely, forgiveness. For in order that this may come about a man must confess his wrong to another, and this injures his pride and sense of deserving. And yet our whole life together as a community wins freedom and happiness just from the fact that one is prepared to confess his sin to another and to receive forgiveness.
<|det|>text [111, 608, 934, 809]<|/det|>In just the same way men spoil their relation to God because they are not prepared to confess what they truly are in the sight of God: worthless creatures who have nothing which they did not receive from Him, creatures whose lives are without any content or meaning apart from His grace, and who must flee again and again to His grace in order to have any value, creatures who can receive from Him alone that meaningfulness which frees them from the tense struggle to assert their value.
