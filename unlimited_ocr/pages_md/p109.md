<|det|>text [58, 92, 885, 479]<|/det|>Joy! light! Who does not wish it for himself! Of course at a time like the present we feel very strongly that it must be no ordinary joy—not the kind of rejoicing in which we might forget what is happening around us and with us, no joy which might lull us into forgetfulness of the heavy hardships we are undergoing, hardships which forbid all frivolous delights, no joy which would drown our remembrance of those who are fighting for us, and in face of whom we at home ask ourselves the question whether we have any right to rejoice at all, or to wish joy for ourselves. No! it must be no ordinary joy but it must at least be a joy which we would share in common with our fighting men, and which would link us with them. But in any case it cannot be joy of an ordinary kind. For how could any ordinary joy last in face of the distresses of the time? Such joy would be no more than the frivolous pleasures of two or three fleeting days, after which all would seem dark again around us and within us.
<|det|>text [58, 480, 885, 770]<|/det|>No, it can be no ordinary joy! It would at least have to be a joy which had the power to predominate over anxiety and grief, and to become within us a living source of power to transform the darkness around and within into light. No ordinary joy, which again means no joy such as earthly things confer. It would have to be eternal supernatural joy. And this is what it is to be! The message of Christmas speaks of precisely such a joy; and for this heavenly joy are we to prepare ourselves in the season of Advent. We are to move towards an encounter with Him who wishes us to come to Him, and who says: “I am the light of the world. He who follows me will not walk in darkness but will have the light of life.”
