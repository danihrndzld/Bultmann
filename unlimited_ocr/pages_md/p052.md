But the “no longer” theme is heard when we look upwards to Christ and to His grace which frees us and comes to redeem us in all our joy and bitter sorrow. And then the “not yet” theme merges into a triumphant “but already”. “But already!” for divine grace accepts us as those whom we are not in our own strength, but whom we are to be and would wish to be; it accepts us as those who have been justified by faith. It fills our impoverished souls with its riches, it helps our weakness with its power, it stills our fears with its consolations, it forgives our guilt with its love. “But already”—against the triumphant background of the “but already”, the “not yet” loses its pain. “Not yet” certainly, but “I press on to make it my own, because Jesus Christ has made me His own.”
<|det|>text [106, 384, 934, 722]<|/det|>It is especially plain to us to-day that the future requires from us courage and calm. For however obscure it may be for us, we do know that it will demand sacrifices. Now when Christ has truly laid His grip upon us, we experience not only the power of His resurrection, but also the fellowship of His sufferings. Then in times of stress and crisis it will be seen whether we really endured the marks of His mastery of us, and were willing to allow Him to set us free from the burden of ourselves. Can we step out into the future as those who have been set free, “undismayed and without flinching”? Are we ready to receive His grace which liberates us in that it ever anew breaks our lives, shatters over and over again the old man which constantly clings to us? Shatters us with our pride and our indolence, our tense and gnawing self-concern and, on the other hand, our frivolity?
<|det|>text [106, 725, 938, 904]<|/det|>Looking back on the term that is now ended, may our experiences teach us to endure the pressure of the touch of Christ; may the gift of the term to us be that we who are weak are learning to let His grace make us strong, that we are coming to know the power of His resurrection and the fellowship of His sufferings, that we are pressing on “toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus”.
