<|det|>text [120, 81, 949, 442]<|/det|>No doubt we all long for a joy which no man can take from us. But do we wish for it in all seriousness? If so, then we must realize this: that we are aspiring to a joy which does not cling to this world. We must become clear about this noteworthy fact, namely that no one can tell us what is the true object of such joy. No one can tell us at what, on this level, we can and should rejoice. For everything that might be named as its object would still belong to this old world. And if for many centuries the pious have dreamed of such joy in their fanciful pictures of the heavenly city with its golden streets, and of the choirs and songs of the new Jerusalem where the blessed live for evermore, we must say that all such pictures are mere images and symbols for a joy that is inexpressible. For the ineffable joy which no man can take from us, no object can be named in human terms and concepts.
