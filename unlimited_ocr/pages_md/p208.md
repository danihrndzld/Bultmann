<|det|>text [112, 334, 939, 604]<|/det|>The spiritual strength of Paul springs from the fact that he lives in two worlds; not only in the visible world of change and decay, of tears and death, that world in which we are “afflicted in every way” and “perplexed”, but also in the invisible world in which there is no fear and no despair. From this invisible world he derives the power to master all those pains and trials which flow from the visible and temporal. In fact we may go further and add that the trials of the visible and temporal scene serve to make him all the more certain of invisible realities: “We have this treasure in earthen vessels, to show that the transcendent power belongs to God and not to us.”
<|det|>text [112, 605, 941, 901]<|/det|>The fact that man lives in two worlds is not a distinctively Christian insight. Everywhere and at every time in which man has transcended his anxiety about his bodily existence, about the material security of his life, has opened the “eye of the soul” and in inner spiritual vision has caught a glimpse of the world of the spirit, then he has lived in two worlds and then two worlds have dwelt in his breast. At such moments the world of the spirit, the world of beauty, truth and goodness shines upon him in its inspiring splendour and instils into him the sense of utter obligation towards it.
