<|det|>text [111, 335, 941, 901]<|/det|>What then is the right way of prayer? We need not speak much longer on this theme, for the fact is that we have been speaking of it essentially all the time! “If you ask anything of the Father, He will give it to you in my name. Hitherto you have asked nothing in my name.” The right way of prayer is prayer in the name of Christ. The disciples had prayed much and often already before the passion and Easter, but not “in His name”. They had prayed: “Thy kingdom come, give us this day our daily bread” and all that belongs to this context. They are not now to offer a different prayer, but they are to pray differently. “In the name of Jesus” means in the light of His cross and His victory over the world. No doubt they are meant to pour forth to God the sorrows and the desires of their heart, but as men who are inwardly prepared for renunciation and sacrifice, and who are therefore without fear or dismay. Can then the promise that God will grant us our requests simply mean that we can count on the fulfilment of all our wishes? Certainly not! Yet in a deeper sense, yes. For if we pray in His name, that is, in freedom from the bondage of self, then in all that we ask for we are at bottom praying for encounter with God, for the life which God bestows. To pray in the name of Jesus means to pray as Jesus prayed in the garden of Gethsemane, to accompany every prayer by the qualification: “Nevertheless not my will, but Thine, be done.”
