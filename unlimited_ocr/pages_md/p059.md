<|det|>text [62, 272, 882, 563]<|/det|>But the New Testament speaks otherwise than the poets about man's strangeness and homelessness in the world. It does not sound the note of longing, born of weariness and disillusionment or of surfeit, for there the challenge to the world is sounded as a result of the intervention of God's power. This challenge is not to be understood in the sense that the Christian faith intrudes to set the world and its problems to rights. But on the other hand it would also be a misunderstanding to suppose that the Christian gospel called men out of the world, so that they no longer had any share in either its gifts or its tasks. It is a question of one thing only: to say to the world that it is not the final power which can claim dominion over men.
