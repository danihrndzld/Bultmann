<|det|>text [55, 158, 881, 473]<|/det|>If this is so, then it is clear that belief in God as Creator and belief in His wonder-working action and energy is not a simple and easy matter, but a belief that is extremely difficult and hardly possible to come by, and for that very reason it is the fundamental thing. In fact belief in God as Creator contains in essence the whole Christian faith. Luther says in commenting on the creation story: “For doubtless the most important article of the faith is that in which we declare: I believe in God, Father Omnipotent, Creator of heaven and earth. And whoever truly believes this, has already been helped and restored to the right way and has reached the state from which Adam fell. But there are few people who have progressed so far as to believe in the fullest sense that our God is the God who creates and makes all things.” \( ^{1} \)

Does the unfolding course of events in world history always strike us as being grounded in the wondrous activity of God? We may well no doubt have moments such as those we mentioned earlier in which we believe we see the miracles which God effects. But do these moments give light to our whole life, so that we always realize ourselves to be held in God's creator hand? Nay, do they retain their sway when our passions overwhelm us or when we are struck by a fate which threatens to break us utterly? The beautiful and uplifting things we have experienced may well sound a comforting echo and prove a precious possession in times of hardship and distress.
