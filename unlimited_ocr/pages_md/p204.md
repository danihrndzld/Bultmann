<|det|>text [113, 170, 934, 415]<|/det|>“Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God.” The peacemakers are those who are always ready to maintain peace or to lay the foundations of peace; those who, with their tranquil trust in God, are so inwardly detached from what they possess or control that they are not anxiously concerned about their rights, nor do they obstinately insist on their rights. Their inner disposition is so noble that they are ready to relax their claims; they can smilingly let slide what to others seems immensely important, and with a smile and a shrug they can bear to be called weak.
<|det|>text [113, 418, 934, 664]<|/det|>And finally: “Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness’ sake, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.” Those who are inwardly detached from the transitory will be able with their whole power to devote themselves to the things that are abiding, to the beautiful, the true and the good, and in so doing they will be without anxiety about any suffering that may befall them. In the service of the eternal and the unseen they gladly take upon themselves the burden of hatred and persecution. Whenever it is a question of God or the world, they are unhesitating in making a decision which brings them freedom.
<|det|>text [113, 666, 934, 844]<|/det|>What is promised to the merciful, to the pure in heart, to the peacemakers, to those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake, is always the same: they shall obtain mercy, they shall see God, they shall be called the children of God, the kingdom of heaven is theirs. All this has the same meaning. God prevents them and holds them in His everlasting love, draws them into fellowship with Himself, and bestows upon them that which brings fulfilment to their lives.
