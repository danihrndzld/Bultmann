<|det|>text [96, 251, 923, 906]<|/det|>Naturally this is not an easy panacea for our ills, as though everyone who is having a bad time here could wait to be richly compensated in the heavenly glory which shall be. Only he can be comforted and only he can confess the faith who has clearly understood that his present life is apparent and unreal if it is not even now determined and sustained by that future for which he hopes, and that his present life is imbued with power and truth only if he wills to be what he is intended to be, only if he really lives by the inspiration of the future. What does this mean? It may in some sense be said that all men live by the inspiration of the future, that they do not wish to be what they are in fact here and now, but rather what they hope to become. This is true of all those who are striving in human affairs after any kind of goal, whether great or small; whether it be the security of a profession, or the quiet of family life, whether it be recognition and honour, whether it be power in economic or political life, whether it be knowledge or the creative achievement of the artist. At bottom all these men live in the strength of what they have not yet, what they feel is yet to come, the future goal of their hopes and strivings; it is this which gives their life its true content and inspiring energy. None of them can say to the passing moment: “Do tarry, thou art so beautiful.” And if perhaps in some moment of good fortune and bliss we feel inclined to say this, yet it is only because this moment is an earnest of the future. Now this means that all life which is aiming to reach a goal is always only a provisional, unfulfilled and relative existence. What man wishes essentially to be lies ever ahead of him.
