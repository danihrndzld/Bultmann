And we shall understand too that what was bestowed on us was not bestowed to make us linger over it but to make us look forward to the future, that it might bear fruit in the future.
<|det|>text [48, 158, 867, 562]<|/det|>The purpose of the gift was to make us more mature and capable for the carrying out of future tasks. In a twofold way. Firstly everything that has come our way should inspire us with courage and strength to meet what the future holds, should fill us with aspiration to live a nobler and purer life, and should make us ready both for action and sacrifice. But in such a way that we never forget that “in our own strength we can accomplish nothing, soon we stumble and fall”. What we now have and are, is His gift, and also what we shall become will be His gift. And our readiness for action and sacrifice will only be maintained and come to good effect if it implies readiness to live by the gift of His grace, to allow His grace, to allow Him to act upon us and within us, as He wills; and this means readiness to abandon ourselves as it were to that void in which there is no support and no security, in which we are held only by the grip of Him who in our recent past has thrust with power and grace into our lives.

<|det|>text [48, 635, 867, 905]<|/det|>But perhaps there are some among us who do not dare to say that they can detect the grip of Christ upon them in the experiences which the term now ending has brought. Perhaps there are some who do not look back with glad and grateful hearts, but with disillusionment and dismay. What has the term brought? Increase of knowledge perhaps, but knowledge without inspiration or power. Perhaps new insight, yes, but a painful insight, the discovery of the insecurity of what seemed secure. Doubt about what seemed certain and in consequence doubt about the meaning and worthwhileness of our work. Or did the term bring neglected opportunities and wasted hours? Doubt about ourselves?
