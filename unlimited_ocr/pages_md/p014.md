<|det|>text [106, 38, 926, 129]<|/det|>Moreover we are not speaking of those outside our own circle, outside the Church and the Christian faith. No, we all belong integrally to modern life and are all subject to this temptation.
<|det|>text [106, 130, 926, 584]<|/det|>The declaration that God is Judge calls our attention to the fact that all the phenomena of life are ambiguous and hence sources of temptation, the fact that we may use them for good or lose our souls to them. But we do in fact lose our souls to them if we believe them to be unequivocal, thus ceasing to feel their power to tempt and lead astray. We owe an account as to what we make of them. This however applies above all to ourselves, to the energies and impulses, the gifts and the passions of our inner being. These latter are by no means unequivocal, and not everything that springs from the instincts of blood and nature is good. Primitive man is aware of the frailty and dubiety of his being, for he is aware of its dangers and does not suppose that he is master of himself. The German poet too knows this: “Ah! how little we know ourselves, for a divine force lives and moves within us” (Hölderlin). In a very popular book of our time, the Majorin \( ^{1} \) of Ernst Wiechert, the author shows most convincingly and vividly that men know themselves so little and then are terrified at themselves when unsuspected forces burst forth from the inner heart of their lives.
<|det|>text [106, 585, 930, 744]<|/det|>But modern man thinks he knows himself, or he lives as if he did. He can do this only because he has forgotten the truth: “In Him we live and move and have our being.” For this word warns him that he lives his life before the eyes of a judge, to whom he is always responsible, always transparent. “Whither shall I go from Thy Spirit, or whither shall I flee from Thy presence?”
<|det|>text [106, 745, 930, 881]<|/det|>Modern man supposes that he is not ambivalent in the impulses and stirrings of his blood. How seldom now are we terrified at ourselves, how seldom do we shudder to contemplate our own thoughts and deeds! We are rather accustomed to praise and extol our thoughts and deeds when they lead to achievements which are visible to all.
