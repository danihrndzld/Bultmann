<|det|>text [65, 34, 890, 606]<|/det|>The effort of all men is directed towards the banishment of fear, towards making life transparently clear and controllable in the interests of our desires and plans, towards the ever-growing strength of human self-security. We are determined that nothing more shall disturb us or make us afraid. The modern world has witnessed an astonishing development of technics and has harnessed technics in the service of this constant aim. In this mastery of the world by technical means we see the ever-present temptation to regard our life as assured, to regard the world as the sphere of our domination, and to forget the original uncertainty and questionableness of the structure of our existence: just as the rich man whose land brought forth plentifully supposed that the mere possession of the means of subsistence had also made his life as a whole secure. But God said to him: "Fool! This night your soul is required of you!" In fact there is no need to dwell on the point that, in spite of all the perfection of our technology, nature itself is never fully within man's control, and that its forces again and again break forth with destructive violence in natural catastrophes and are constantly evident in tragedy and misfortune. Every newspaper we pick up bears witness to this. Nevertheless, the general sense of security is so great that the most insignificant reader, while being moved, says to himself: that sort of thing cannot happen to me.
<|det|>text [65, 606, 890, 901]<|/det|>But there is one fact in life which is not to be overcome by technical means: namely, death. We do not like to speak of it, as in general we anxiously avoid mentioning what is unpleasant and repulsive to us: such things might disturb our complacency! For this reason we think and speak as little as possible of this greatest disturbance of all, death. Formerly it was regarded as a matter of course that man should prepare himself for death, when it became imminent, and that in such circumstances he should look to the Church for comfort and consolation. To-day we try to conceal from people as much as possible the approach of their end. Why did people behave differently in former times?
