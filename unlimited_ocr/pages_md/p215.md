<|det|>text [58, 201, 881, 539]<|/det|>A period of history in which the spirit has been despised and the word of God's grace reviled lies behind us. The world of the spirit has once more gained an opportunity of expansion, and the word of God's grace may be freely proclaimed. And even now we occasionally have the shaming experience of hearing people complain, in face of the sufferings which we are still undergoing and must undergo for some time to come, that we were better off in the days of the old régime and should be better off now if it were still in force. We meet people who would be ready to exchange the intellectual freedom which has been restored to us, the freedom from falsehood and infamy, for material well-being. This attitude is surely the lowest stage to which a man can sink. No! “Man shall not live by bread alone but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God” (Matt. 4: 4).
<|det|>text [58, 540, 881, 809]<|/det|>But the truth is that distress and want have hit people so hard that we understand when they despair, and we do not wish to condemn them harshly. And our challenge: do we wish to live in the world of the spirit?—might well be put as follows: are we able to live in that world? Is not the life of so many of our people who have lost all their possessions and their loved ones, but a hollow worthless shell? Are not all their thoughts and abilities so fully claimed and absorbed by the effort to secure the barest necessities of life that no time or power is left them to live in the world of the spirit? Are we not threateningly faced by the coming winter which will surely bring us cold and hunger?
