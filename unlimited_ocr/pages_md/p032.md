<|det|>text [92, 308, 923, 761]<|/det|>AFTER many cold and depressing weeks, the sun of spring has begun to cheer us with its radiance and warmth. The vital forces of nature, long frozen and paralysed, have at last burst forth freely; trees and bushes have adorned themselves with the splendour of their blossom, and the trees are once more green. As the farmer in the surrounding country has been glad to be able to get down to his work in earnest, we town-dwellers too have felt an impulse of grateful joy. Our work, of course, unlike that of the farmer, is not dependent on the weather; nevertheless, we rejoice once more in the coming of the spring. We rejoice not merely because the prosperity of all of us depends on the undisturbed continuance of the processes of nature so that the earth may bring forth its fruit. Our own pleasure in the spring is much more immediate and instinctive, and needs no special justification. And, too, the more our lives are harnessed to work, responsibility and care, the more we feel it as a release to be able to open ourselves freely to the light and beauty of the spring, when there takes place a veritable renaissance in the world of nature which speaks to us with an audible voice.
<|det|>text [96, 763, 929, 901]<|/det|>No doubt there are people who do not hear this voice and do not feel things in this way. But feeling certain that our joy in the spring is right and healthy we know that there is something lacking to these people, that there is something morose and crabbed about them and that this lack infects the whole of their lives.
