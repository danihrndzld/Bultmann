<|det|>text [96, 42, 932, 359]<|/det|>How could the words: “forgetting what lies behind and straining forward” be appropriate in such a case? Does not such a man find it easier to turn away from his past? Has not the temptation of self-complacency been spared him? No, he has encountered it in a far more dangerous guise. Precisely such a judgment about his past, a judgment made in the bitterness of disillusionment or despair, in a far more sinister way fetters a man to his past, to himself and to the old Adam. Such a one is tempted to cling tenaciously to his disillusionment, his bitterness, his self-condemnation as though it were a sour but precious fruit. He torments himself perversely as long as he does not realize that precisely in this disillusionment and doubt and despair Christ is thrusting into his life to free him from the bonds of self.
<|det|>text [102, 361, 939, 906]<|/det|>But how shall we show him that it is none other than the grip of Christ under which he suffers, and that this painful grip is aiming to free him from his tormented self? “Is this the touch of Christ?” he will ask, and we shall counter by asking him, “How can it not be?” If you are honest with yourself, can you ever free yourself from this word which has pierced your life like a sword, and from this experience which has intruded upon it with disturbing force? Does it not awaken in you an unceasing unrest, as it awakens unrest in the world at large which can never be again as it once was, before the word and message of Christ resounded in it? in the world which again to-day is so obviously striving to resist the thrust of Christ who wishes to shatter its pride and so free it from its torment? Is not your dispeace due precisely to this—that you have encountered the disturbing force of the word of Christ, so that you are driven to confess: “It was easier and would be easier without this intrusive touch”? “Were it not for this intrusive word, then life might be mastered, for then it would be a closed and secure circle”? Do you look back with nostalgic regret to the time before this strange disturbance, as we look back with longing to the time of childhood?
