<|det|>text [106, 111, 923, 627]<|/det|>How many a morning of course steals upon us, grey with cloud and mist! And it is not always easy for us to assimilate the blessing of the new day. But how desirable it would be if we could do so always with a moment of silent meditation, a prayer or a hymn, whether alone or in fellowship with the other members of our family. It would be so salutary for our mood during the day's work, for the way in which we approach the people with whom we have dealings, if only each day began with quiet self-recollection in the presence of God, with thanksgiving and petition. How often do we begin the day quite thoughtlessly, and hardly have we awakened from sleep when we are again caught up in the cares and the business of life. We can see from the behaviour of people how they have begun their day; many a popular expression brings home the same truth. But even when we have had a time of quiet and prayer in the early morning, how often it is quickly forgotten in the bustle of the new day! How grateful we should be for Sunday which brings an interruption to the work of the week, which stands out as a day of rest from the agitation and tumult of our week's work, which invites us to pause and reflect in God's presence, when the bells ring out with their appeal: "Come to me, all who labour and are heavy laden."
<|det|>text [106, 630, 923, 897]<|/det|>Are we not all weary and heavy laden? Laden with the burdens of the week, and the grime of everyday toil? What a load of trouble and care each week brings, what petty and exhausting tasks, oftentimes frustrating labour! And this is the case even when we are engaged in work which delights us and expresses our sense of personal vocation. Even the most fruitful labour is in the long run undermining and fatiguing, and we notice this at the end of the week. And we notice it still more if at any time we have spent a Sunday without due rest, whether from frivolous motives, or because on Sunday there was so much to get done which in the week we had to set aside.
