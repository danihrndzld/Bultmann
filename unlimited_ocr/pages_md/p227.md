<|det|>text [73, 207, 896, 546]<|/det|>What then is the ultimate source of the distress and suffering in which we are involved? Is it ultimately the fault of the conquerors that so many of us are without homes and shelter? Are they responsible for the fact that we go hungry? We do not propose in this place to discuss in all its breadth and scope the vexed question of guilt. It is certain, however, that not every one of us is guilty to the same degree, and that there are many innocent people who have been swept into this tide of bitter pain and suffering for which they are in no wise responsible. It is also certain that the question of guilt bears differently on German youth than on the older generation; for the latter the situation is much more oppressive because on them lies the responsibility for youth, and for the young our common distress is much more terrible than for us older people.
<|det|>text [70, 547, 896, 886]<|/det|>It is not our business here to discuss in general terms the question of guilt in so far as this is a political question, a question to be treated as between human beings. There is only one thing we have to say on the point here and now: it is that only patience and inner calm of mind can and should render possible an objective consideration of the matter. We shall never come to an understanding with each other if bitterness and passion prevail. Having said that, we must add: we stand before the sight of God, and in the eyes of the Most Holy no one of us is pure. Everyone is in some way a participant and somehow responsible when in our nation things are not as they should be.
