<|det|>text [100, 165, 926, 435]<|/det|>This gives us the picture of the community in fate, implied by discipleship to Jesus Christ. And this is our situation as a Christian community, as it was that of the disciples in the hour when Jesus spoke to them His parting words. So long as He dwelt among them, they had not truly grasped the essence of this their situation; even in this solemn hour they still do not realize what it means to belong to Him. They will realize it only after He has left them. “None of you asks me: ‘Where are you going?’” Their minds are fixed not on the whither, the goal of His journey, but on the here and now which He is leaving behind. They will be without Him, bereft and forsaken in the world.
<|det|>text [101, 437, 928, 684]<|/det|>"Nevertheless I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away." Why is it to their advantage? Because they will not become deeply aware of their inner strength and victory over the world so long as they have not experienced their forsakenness in the world and the world's hostility. Their victory? Ah, no! but the victory of Christ. Yet the victory of Christ is to become their victory in virtue of their discipleship to Him, their union with Him. "It is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Counsellor will not come to you; but if I go I will send Him to you."
<|det|>text [104, 686, 931, 913]<|/det|>The Counsellor is none other than the Spirit of Christ, the power which He bestows on those who belong to Him. But they can receive the Spirit only when they have tasted the bitterness of their abandonment. That divine power will make itself felt in the weakness of the desolate; only out of the sadness of their desolation in the world can they learn to know the full measure of the victory over the world which has been vouchsafed them. "When the Spirit of truth comes, He will guide you into all the truth." He will lead them to understand and be assured of their victory over the world
