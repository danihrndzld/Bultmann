<|det|>text [62, 138, 888, 496]<|/det|>Yes, do we know any higher life? Do we know anything of the future, of the transcendent? If we are honest we must admit that we have no direct experience of it, and we know nothing about it. What we do know, and what we have immediate experience of, is ever and only our life up to the present; our life here below, the life of our earthly destiny, the life of our plans and projects. But to reckon with eternity means just this: to view our familiar life as a provisional, unfulfilled and unfulfillable life, and to live it in preparation for a future fulfilled and true life which God wills to bestow on us. If we were to try to picture the future to ourselves, we should only fill it out with the images suggested by our wishes and dreams. Now it is just these fantasies which we are to surrender. To be ready for God's future means to walk trustfully into the darkness, ready for the purposes which God in His wisdom designs us to fulfil.
<|det|>text [58, 498, 886, 905]<|/det|>This future which flows from God does not however meet us only at the end of our days, but he who listens to the divine call may now already and ever afresh experience the way in which God makes him purer and stronger, more serene and cheerful, how God fulfils the prayer: “Make me pure, tender-hearted, detached from the things of sense, calm and gentle, poised in the peace which passes understanding!” The man who has cultivated this preparedness for God’s call in the course of his earthly life will also find himself prepared for the final call of God summoning him out of this our familiar world into the darkness of the unknown. The fact that this final summons will at some time or other be sounded for us, forms the greatest disturbance of all. How few men there are for whom this last call of God is not the most unwelcome of their whole experience! For how few does it come at the right time!
