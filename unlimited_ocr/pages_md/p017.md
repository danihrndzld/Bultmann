The truth is that man stands before God alone. God as Judge tears man away from all his natural contexts and sets him in stark loneliness. Man cannot allow himself to be driven on by the natural instincts ingrained in his nature, but must choose his way in responsibility and decision. This is the truth behind the words: 'We are indeed His offspring.' We are not nature but Spirit; man has a conscience which calls him away into solitude.
<|det|>text [58, 264, 875, 740]<|/det|>"In Him we live and move and have our being": for natural man this word, understood in its true sense, is a terrifying word, a word which shatters all peace and security as everyone can know who realizes what it means to live one's life as in the presence of God. But equally terrible is the ingenuity with which man, by the illusions of his piety, conceals from himself the living God who is Lord of heaven and earth. "O eternity, stupendous word!" we have been singing. But how ingenious is modern man to misuse the word "eternal" as a transfiguring description of the achievements of mankind. On the gate of the churchyard of my native town there stands an old inscription: "O eternity, ever abiding!" Well, recently I read something written on this motto as a theme, and interpreting it as a guiding and encouraging motif for our cultural achievements. What does this mean? It means that the eternity of God which limits and judges our human time—that this eternity is claimed by man for the religious enhancement of his doings and that man, forgetful of the judgment implied in the dimension of eternity, wishes to give to his own actions and achievements the honour which rightly belongs to God alone.

For God requires this of man, that he should not exploit the divine for the enhancement of his own human prestige and glory, but that he should submit to the Lord and give Him the honour which is His due.
