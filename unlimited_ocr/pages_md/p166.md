<|det|>text [111, 133, 930, 583]<|/det|>It would be an obvious answer to say that we should not isolate this parable and view it as a detached saying. We must complete it. We must take it together with the other sayings of Jesus which speak of God as loving Father, with the words of grace and mercy. Of course we must do so. But the composite view must not be so constructed that we complete one feature by adding another, and so make for ourselves a selective picture of Jesus and His message in which one feature tones down another, in which the greatness and the power of the one is weakened by the other, so that finally there issues a picture of Jesus which is muddled and diluted; a picture which at bottom is shaped according to our own tastes and wishes. No; the composite whole must emerge in a different manner. It must arise from a process in which one saying explains another, in which we understand one word in the light of another, so that in the end every word of Jesus is seen to disclose the one and the same Lord, whose word is at one and the same time both terrifying and consoling, a dismaying and a glad message. Only then have we understood it fully.
