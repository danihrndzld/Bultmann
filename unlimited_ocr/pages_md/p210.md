<|det|>text [111, 447, 941, 875]<|/det|>This description of the true sage is of course not yet the description of the true Christian as the words of Paul picture it for us. Yet there exists between the two ideals a characteristic affinity; it is the affinity between idealism and Christianity. A dangerous affinity, we may and indeed must say! For it has often led to a confusion between Christianity and idealism, and for this reason, especially in recent times, it has caused the Church to attack idealism. But to-day above all it would be disastrous, just because of this danger, to fail to recognize this inner kinship. Let us consider. Was it not one and the selfsame time in which Nietzsche's cry rang out: "God is dead!" and in which faith in the world of the spirit died in our culture? The gospel addresses itself to man; but what alone makes man truly human is his participation in the world of the spirit. And as, in the Pauline phrase, the Mosaic law was formerly a "custodian until Christ came", so also is idealism. Naturally, as the Judaic law was misused by man to enable him to establish his own righteousness in the sight of God, the same possibility of abuse exists with regard to idealism.
