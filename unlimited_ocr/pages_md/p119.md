But we must not forget that there are situations in which a despised and outcast person can shame us, so that he suddenly emerges as great and we appear small in comparison. Jesus is fond of reminding His hearers of situations like this. Just such a situation is provided by the story of the Good Samaritan: the despised and outcast Samaritan puts to shame the priest and Levite. Most strange and scandalous! And here too: the despised Publican towers high above the highly respected Pharisee conscious of his rectitude. Most strange and scandalous!
<|det|>text [52, 282, 876, 755]<|/det|>Of course Jesus did not praise the Samaritan because he was a Samaritan, but because he was merciful. Nor does He here put the Publican above the Pharisee because he was a Publican. He does not propose either to condemn the pious zeal of the Pharisee, or to exonerate the vices incidental to the calling of the Publican. But the vision of Jesus pierces deeper than that superficial view of things with which we are usually content. He sees that the true essential being of man lies at a deeper level than that attitude which has become normal for him as the member of a particular profession or social class or even nation, whether good or bad. Yes, the real being of a man lies on a deeper level. There are situations in which it comes to light and those are the really decisive situations. As in the story of the Good Samaritan the situation is that a man sees someone lying wounded in a sinister area and on a dangerous road, and it is then revealed whether he realizes what is the meaning of love of one's neighbour, so here the situation is that of prayer which discloses whether one realizes what is man's true relation to God or not; whether man is inwardly arrogant or humble, whether he—as we may further say—knows himself or is self-deceived.
