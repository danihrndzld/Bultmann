For while we live we are always being given up to death for Jesus' sake, so that the life of Jesus may be manifested in our mortal flesh." And in these verses there is another thing to be observed: all that Paul has previously named, affliction and perplexity, persecution and assault, is now included under one heading: "Always carrying in the body the death of Jesus." That is to say that he understands all our sufferings in the light of the cross. For Paul the cross of Christ is not simply an historical event belonging to a remote past, but an event which, initiated in Christ, penetrates the whole of human history; it is the event in which God pronounces this whole world here below, with all its pride and pageantry, all its cruel distress and pain, to be as nothing. This dying of Christ is repeated wherever a man understands that his suffering is meant to serve the purpose of reminding him that this present world is provisional only and ultimately doomed to destruction. And the resurrection life of Christ is actualized wherever man takes up the cross of Christ, which means that he refuses to cling to this-worldly realities, that he lets them slide in order that God may bestow upon him the life of the transcendent world of the spirit.
<|det|>text [55, 540, 874, 901]<|/det|>Hence all our suffering is meant to cause us to become inwardly released from the fascination of the this-worldly and the visible, to become wholly abandoned to the self-bestowing grace of God—that grace which wills to confer upon us true eternal life. This is the life of which we can have no conception; for we can conceive only a life according to the realities of this world. Nevertheless we are permitted to glimpse something of that higher life just because we live here in two worlds, because, while through the body we are involved in the visible, we may raise our hearts and minds to the world of the spirit and of grace. "But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, to show that the transcendent power belongs to God and not to us."
