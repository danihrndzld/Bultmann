<|det|>text [43, 498, 870, 907]<|/det|>THE story of the Pharisee and the Publican may seem to us a very simple one which everybody can understand immediately, and the truth of which is transparently clear to all. And yet we ought to guard against the danger of taking it in too simple a sense and regarding its truth too much as a matter of course. Certainly in detail there is little to explain. The story is not strictly speaking a parable; for nothing is here compared. Parable is here used, as often in the New Testament, in a broad sense, that of an instructive story simply; and the story presents the contrasting figures of the Pharisee and the Publican as types so that we should ask ourselves: which of these types do we resemble? The Pharisee is the model of correct and zealous piety which in its observance of the law does too much rather than too little; the Publican is a despised figure who stands in close relation with foreigners, with Romans, and lives a dubious existence commonly disparaged as a life of fraud and trickery.
