<|det|>text [106, 48, 933, 501]<|/det|>He will be perceptive of the wonders wrought by God and he will himself do marvellous things. He hears the command of Jesus: "Put out into the deep and let down your nets for a catch." His own deeds appear to him in a new light; they are no longer the deeds which he has himself chosen to do and which he accomplishes in his own strength and by his own planning, they are rather deeds done at God's command, and hence deeds sustained and completed by divine grace. In fact he knows that he can dare to do more than human calculation is able to foresee, for he is independent of what may be attainable according to human calculations, and he commits the results of his doing into God's hands. But his action, flowing from the experienced love of God, is itself an action inspired by love, and so he has the power to bear witness to the love of God before others and to embody God's love in concrete reality. A man whose whole being and conduct is sustained by the peace and joy of love, is himself a miracle in the eyes of others. And in this sense we are all called not only to behold the wonders of God but on our part to effect wondrous things.
<|det|>text [106, 502, 936, 889]<|/det|>My friends, this is the last academic service of the present term and it presents us—especially the academic community which is to-day gathered here—with a serious question. This term we have been allowed to pursue our studies in peace, while so many of our fellow-students, colleagues and friends are at the front, torn from their normal work, living in stress and danger, and ensuring by the devotion of their energies and lives the peace of our quiet round of study. We think of them now in the deep bonds of fellowship and gratitude. But we must also remember that for us it is an undeserved grace of God that we have been able to carry on our work here. Ought we not to interpret this also as a miracle that has been granted us? Hence let us shoulder our responsibilities all the more seriously and bear witness by our own weak action to the mighty working of God, so that even in these distressing and anxious times the Creator's praise may ring out. "The Lord's name be praised!"
