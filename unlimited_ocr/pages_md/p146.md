We have no time, our work is so pressing! We are constantly expected to give out and we have not enough ourselves. It is true that there is so much distress that we cannot help everybody. But have we always done as much as we could? Have we at least always had a friendly glance, a kind word when we were approached? Or if anyone wants anything from us, do we always feel it to be a horrid disturbance?

<|det|>text [96, 433, 927, 750]<|/det|>But it is not merely the need of our fellow men in which God's call to us is heard. It may also be our own need, our own fate, which strikes us down and makes us shiver with dismay. When our lives go according to our plans and wishes, when life brings attractive work for our powers, and when our work is crowned with success and joy, then our world seems to be irradiated as from within by its own light. In such circumstances it is easy for us to forget that the light which illuminates our path really falls on our world from without, that it is the light of the goodness of God, that it streams upon us as sunlight on the earth. And just as the sun can be hidden behind clouds as the sun sets, so also our world gladdened by congenial tasks and the pride of success may once again be plunged in darkness.
<|det|>text [96, 751, 930, 910]<|/det|>When this happens our world can grow sinister and uncanny, and the activities of all men including our own begin to seem strange and purposeless. If such a fate strikes us, in one way or another, then it is God who is confronting us anew. But shall we be able to recognize Him in this mysterious darkness?
