<|det|>text [106, 314, 932, 539]<|/det|>Do we belong to the number of those who suffer in this world? Those whose vision penetrates beyond the sphere of the visible to that of the eternal and invisible? Are we among those who hope and wait in expectancy for the One who is to come? This is the critical Advent question. If we do belong to this number then we must in our turn, like the Baptist, address to Jesus the question: “Are you the One who is to come?” Is Jesus Christ in truth the One in whom all our hopes and longings find their fulfilment? Is He the one who brings in the kingdom of peace?
<|det|>text [106, 541, 931, 697]<|/det|>Were He the One—then surely He must already have established His kingdom! If He is the destined Messiah, He has come already indeed, long ago. More than 1900 years ago! And has the world changed as a result of His coming? The Christian Church affirms just this: He has come and has ushered in the new age, and yet He is still the One who is to come. In what sense may this be understood?
