<|det|>text [69, 107, 890, 355]<|/det|>Not readiness for the future, but fear of it, is the decisive motivating force in human life. Really to live available for the future would mean to overcome the anguished fear of it in the certainty that whatever it brings can only serve our ultimate good. And the final proof of the Christian attitude would be not to shudder and recoil at the thought of the death which the future will assuredly bring to every one of us, but rather to go forward to meet it undismayed in the assured conviction that “neither death nor life, nor angels nor principalities, nor things present nor things to come, nor powers . . . will be able to separate us from the love of God”.
<|det|>text [69, 357, 890, 895]<|/det|>But who can speak with this degree of assurance? May perhaps he whose aims are not tangible worldly goods, but who strives only to attain the purity and perfection of his own true being? Does not such a one live with a view to a genuine future? Does he not look to a future which can rob him of nothing since he has not set his heart on transitory and tangible things? No doubt he lives by the inspiration of what he wishes to become, yet not in view of the real future which is coming to him. He is living in the strength of his own resources, not by the blessing which the future will bring to him and not in readiness for the purposes which God has for him. But the more he lives by his own strength, the more also he lives enslaved to the past, to what he has made of himself. And the more frankly he confesses that he is not yet what he wishes to become, the more he becomes conscious of the discord in which his life is rooted; he would like to be the man whom he sees before him as a serene and shining ideal, and yet he is ever entrapped by his own past with its half-heartedness and pettiness, its impurity and evil from which he can never free himself. And then his hope cleaves no longer to what he wishes to make of himself, but to the possibility of release and redemption from the fetters of self, and the basic disharmony of his being.
