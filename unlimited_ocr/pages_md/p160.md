<|det|>text [101, 96, 931, 300]<|/det|>In the passage in which Luther says that belief in God as Creator is the central, decisive, all-embracing belief, and in which he adds that there are few “who have progressed so far as to believe in the fullest sense that our God is the God who creates and makes all things”, he continues thus: “For such a man must be dead to all things, to good and ill, to life and death, to heaven and hell, and must from the depths of his heart confess that in his own strength he can do nothing.”
<|det|>text [106, 300, 938, 637]<|/det|>The man who is able to confess this is also able to realize what a gift God has given to the world in Jesus Christ. Such a one encounters Jesus as the manifestation of the redeeming grace of God in this world, and the word which promises this grace to the sinner finds its way into his heart. All this is made vividly clear to us in our story. When Peter in obedience to the command of Jesus has let down his net and made the wonderful catch of fish, he falls to his knees before Jesus and says: “Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, O Lord.” Jesus, however, does not reject his adoration but promises: “Do not be afraid; henceforth you will be catching men.” Thus to the prostrate sinner the miracle is made known, and the real miracle is just this—that Jesus calls to His service sinful man, that He transforms the being of sinful man making him a new and pure creation.
<|det|>text [111, 638, 944, 839]<|/det|>In fact the one and the true miracle is that the word of God's saving grace which meets us in Jesus Christ creates us anew, freeing us from the fetters of ourselves and our old sinful way of life, thereby making us a “new creation”. The fact that this word of grace goes on resounding, that it challenges us even to-day, here and now, with the question whether we are prepared to understand our whole life in the light of this divine word—just that is the revelation to us of God's rule as Creator.
