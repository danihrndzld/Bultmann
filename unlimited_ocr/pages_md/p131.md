<|det|>text [55, 40, 874, 200]<|/det|>What do they tell us? They form part of the seven letters addressed to the seven Christian Churches in Asia Minor in a time of persecution, and they are to be heard by the whole Church. In ever fresh variations they repeat the summons: "I come, I stand at the door!" It is a rousing, soul-stirring summons, and its meaning may be summarized as: "This world is not the sole or ultimate reality."
<|det|>text [55, 201, 874, 606]<|/det|>This our world has its hidden background which we are accustomed to forget. And from that hidden beyond, there can and there will one day break forth into this world a power for judgment or salvation, according to the quality of our preparedness and vigilance. We are not, as we fondly suppose, in sole control of this world but the world and all our activities within it will be brought to account by its Creator and Lord. Remember what confronts all men with imminent judgment! This our world, so familiar and homely as we think, is not after all our true home: we shall be torn from it and it will sink into nothingness for us on the day when we must die. Be mindful of your last day! Our life which we suppose to be our own does not lie within our disposal, has not the unequivocal goal which we assume for it, nor the completeness of meaning which we would like to lend it. A higher hand governs its affairs, unseen, and may at any moment intrude with a painful thrust. Be mindful of the truth that you do not belong to yourselves!
