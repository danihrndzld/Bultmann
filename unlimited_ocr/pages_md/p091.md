No doubt the result will be different in each individual case if we weigh things up and see whether the profit or loss has been the greater; very different the measure of joy or sorrow, of thankfulness or bitterness in our hearts. But there can be no doubt of this, namely, that each one of us should be thankful. Even he to whom the term has brought distress and disappointment, and who is haunted by the tormenting sense of the unattained, of having trodden devious paths. He too who would like to echo the words of the poet:
<|det|>text [65, 289, 890, 380]<|/det|>"How precious is the coming day, O my judge! How one dreams of wandering in the mountains and of great things! But the day that has just died is terrible; unwillingly, regretfully we look back on it, as on a rubbish heap."
<|det|>text [65, 380, 890, 695]<|/det|>Should not even such a one feel obliged to be thankful? For if he honestly faces the bitterness of his inner self-judgment, must he not confess that in consequence he should be purer and more experienced? But may he affirm this to himself? Now, it is exactly to such a man that the word applies: "My peace I give you. Not as the world gives, give I to you. Let not your hearts be troubled, neither let them be afraid!" For has he not been pursuing a false peace? A peace such as the world gives and which he sought to attain himself and by his own resources? The peace of God can only be given him; and it will be given when such a person turns his attention away from time and from thoughts of self, and is ready for the peace of eternity: "My peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you!"
<|det|>text [58, 697, 888, 900]<|/det|>But this word of Scripture applies no less to those who are looking back on the term with glad and thankful hearts: on its tasks which inspired increasing ability, on their progress in knowledge and understanding, and on the enrichment of their lives through social intercourse and fellowship with others. They too must take to heart this word of Scripture: "My peace I give you; not as the world gives, give I to you", lest from grateful joy they should fall into complacency and self-satisfaction.
