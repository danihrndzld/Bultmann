What is the invisible reality which stands behind that visible scene? Their triumphant ascendancy over the hostile world. For what invisibly takes place in the unfolding of those terrible visible events of the crucifixion of Jesus and the persecution of the Church, is in truth nothing other than the judgment of the world. “And when He comes He will convince the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment.” What is the meaning of this?
<|det|>text [65, 266, 890, 513]<|/det|>The title which in the Johannine farewell discourses is given to the Spirit has the meaning of helper and advocate; in fact what is really meant is an advocate in a judgment scene. And this is basically the meaning of the remarkable verse: the Spirit discloses to the mind the truth that the events which Jesus and His own experience constitute are in fact the great judgment before the judgment seat of God, in which the Spirit is their advocate. What is here in question is the divine judgment which will not first take place at the end of time, but which is even now being enacted; it is the divine judgment on the world.
<|det|>text [66, 514, 890, 604]<|/det|>The Spirit opens the eyes to this truth; for the world it remains hidden and that is the terrible thing, that the world itself does not know that its judgment is continually taking place.
<|det|>text [65, 604, 890, 899]<|/det|>How does this come about? The poet says: “World history implies world judgment.” But this is not the meaning here. For the poet wishes to affirm that in the course of world history all wrong is avenged, all right finally triumphs, and that the development of world history is an upward progress towards the light. But the gospel does not suggest this. For the Christian faith (and not it alone) knows, or can know, that this belief is an illusion, and that the struggle between right and wrong, between truth and falsehood in world history is never decisively closed, but lasts as long as earth remains.
