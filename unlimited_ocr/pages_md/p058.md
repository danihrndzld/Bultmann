<|det|>text [111, 134, 945, 291]<|/det|>Such is the mysterious sway of the world that it presumes to claim us wholly, so that we are cheated into thinking it the ultimate reality; this is its uncanny tendency, to delude us into thinking that it can satisfy us completely with its gifts, can absorb us utterly with its duties. The consequence is that the world itself becomes a god, rounded in itself, closed and all-sufficient.
<|det|>text [117, 292, 949, 542]<|/det|>But if anyone sets his face against this claim made by the world and refuses to become absorbed in its gifts or demands, if anyone rejects the world's pretension to offer what is finally decisive and important, then the world pursues him with inexorable hatred. This hatred may take various forms: it can assume the mask of seductive amiability, and it can become open hostility and persecution. For if a man repudiates the world's claim to have the power to fill his life completely and utterly absorb his attention in the duties it imposes, then it attempts to make clear to him that at any rate it has the power to destroy him in infamy and torment.
<|det|>text [121, 543, 953, 743]<|/det|>These truths can be felt by anyone who still has an awareness that all the care, all the passion in the world does not in the last resort exhaust the potentiality of his being, anyone who is still capable of not being stunned by clamour and show, and in whom there still lives a nostalgic longing, as it were, for the stars. This the poets also know. The poem which we have just quoted about the fighter, the son of earth, who in the struggles of noonday forgets the stars, continues thus:
