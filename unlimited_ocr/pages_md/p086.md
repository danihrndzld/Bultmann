<|det|>text [100, 42, 927, 291]<|/det|>Oh that we might begin a new life! How different it would be, and how much better. No doubt many a time and in many things we can make a new start by taking a fierce resolution, and we should do so. But does this always succeed? And above all, we cannot do this in the most important matters. For who can silence the voice of conscience, which reminds us of past guilt? Which of us could cleanse ourselves of all the stains which sully our past? Which of us could restore so much that is grievously, irreparably, damaged in our life and community? For how many of us is it true that it is now too late!
<|det|>text [100, 292, 929, 493]<|/det|>Does Sunday spell a new beginning for us in these respects too? We must remember that the Lord's Day is the Day of the resurrection of Christ; the day on which the new world of life in the spirit victoriously broke in upon the old world of sin and death, and so initiated a new beginning for all who honour the Risen Lord as their Saviour and Master. "If any one is in Christ he is a new creation; the old has passed away, behold, the new has come. All this is from God who through Christ reconciled us to Himself."
<|det|>text [106, 496, 930, 903]<|/det|>It is not in our power to make a new beginning, but God has made a new beginning for us. And again and again we can allow this divine initiative to become a reality for us, we can repeatedly be strengthened and refreshed by it. This is the very meaning of God's word which each new Sunday proclaims to us; it warns us that we may and must do this, that our thoughts should not remain centred on our old life but on the grace which God bestows on us in Christ. The bells ring out the message that we are they whose life He has restored, whom He has reconciled to Himself, sinners whom He has forgiven, the impure whom He has made pure. Sunday as the Lord's Day is meant to give us the assurance that we in all our guilt and weakness belong even now to the new world, the world of God and of eternal life.
