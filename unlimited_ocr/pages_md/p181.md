They must be told: if you hope and long for the experience of God's goodness then you yourselves must be ready to exercise goodness. In your lament over your own distresses there must not be mingled any note of complaining that others are better treated than you; there must not be intermixed with your sorrows any suggestion of mean envy. Rather, if we wish to experience joy ourselves, we must be ready to rejoice with others. And it is remarkable how, in our joy over the good fortune of others, our own troubles find some alleviation. If we are able to rejoice with others in their happiness, we inwardly participate in the good which has been bestowed on them. Then we are permitted to see the manifestation of God's goodness, and to become aware of God's goodness in its manifestations is always an enheartening and strengthening experience. And it is just the man who can see God's goodness reflected in the lives of others, who has also eyes to see it when it comes to himself. But if we cannot rejoice in the blessings which enrich other lives, then we make ourselves blind to those which come to ourselves.
