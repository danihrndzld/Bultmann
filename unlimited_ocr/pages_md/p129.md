<|det|>text [58, 39, 881, 380]<|/det|>as he is in his urge to assert his authority and in his pathetic self-deception; and it brings him back to his true self—as he receives himself from God's hand: as the justified man, who in his spiritual freedom does not need to lower his eyes before any man since he has lowered them before God. For such a man the world too, and human society, is transfigured. Instead of an unscrupulous desire to press one's claims at all costs, we have the spirit of sincere, honest and self-forgetful service. Instead of envious ill-will, and ruthlessness, we have a cheerful spirit of mutual regard and esteem. Instead of reserve and tense self-concern, we have friendliness, frankness and joy in fellowship with others. If our parable persuades anyone to adopt this new way of life, on such a one Jesus has exercised His liberating redemptive power.
