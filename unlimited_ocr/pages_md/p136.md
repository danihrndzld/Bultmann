<|det|>text [108, 133, 930, 425]<|/det|>In the life of every man eternity finally intrudes in the form of death. Death stands at the door and knocks some time or other for all of us. The popular legend speaks with deep truth about the messengers of death whom the latter sends in advance to warn people of its imminent approach. These messengers are pain and sorrow, old age and other tokens of our transience. Shall we frivolously disregard them? Or shall we pay heed to them and prepare ourselves for the coming of eternity? Is it not the messengers of death, of eternity, which bring us back to ourselves? Especially is sorrow meant to bring us to ourselves and to enable us to receive that gold which is refined with fire. “Those whom I love, I reprove and chasten; so be zealous and repent.”
<|det|>text [108, 427, 930, 763]<|/det|>Yet it would be wrong to think that only pain and the signs of our transitoriness were the messengers of eternity. There are indeed other messengers and with the blindness of our ingratitude we fail to notice them. They are all the pleasing and cheering things which are sent to us to give us joy. It may be every beam of morning sunlight, every flower which delights us with its beauty and fragrance, every look of love, every kind gift which comes our way. Of course all these things have to be rightly understood and rightly received if they are to be for us messengers of eternity. If ungratefully we take all this happiness and joy for granted as something which we deserve, then we do not understand the message of these things, which is that what gives purest joy and deepest happiness to our lives is always a wondrous gift, a sheer marvel.
