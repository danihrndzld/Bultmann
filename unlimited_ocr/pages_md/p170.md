<|det|>text [102, 101, 928, 463]<|/det|>And how much joy, what stores of blessing, are missed by a nation's youth which supposes that it has a rightful claim to the satisfaction of its wishes! We are of course always hearing it said: "I have a right to my body." No! You have no right to it; your body is a gift entrusted to you by the Creator, a gift which it is your duty to keep whole and pure. And whoever takes as right what should be a gift, inevitably misses the most precious things in life. Naturally readiness for a gift implies also readiness for renunciation. Only he who is prepared to renounce, to surrender all claimfulness, has the power for unhindered receptiveness. And if what he receives is not what he has desired and longed for, then the unexpected gift which is granted him will be for him an unexpected source of blessing and happiness, and he will learn to understand his very renunciation, unavoidable as it was, to be a gift of grace.
<|det|>text [102, 465, 928, 895]<|/det|>In human society there is of course an assertion of right, without which it would be impossible for men to live in community. That, however, is never a question of my right and your right, but of intrinsic rightfulness for which each one must be answerable. And human society can only be sound when the individual is concerned not so much about his own claims as about his duties and obligations. Moreover even this is far too little. Human society subsists not only on a basis of sense of obligation, but also on the basis of love and mutual trust. It can exist and thrive only when the individual is prepared to renounce and sacrifice himself for the sake of another; when its members are not nervously anxious to be uncompromising and unrelaxing in their claims, but rather are anxious to meet the wishes of others, to respect and esteem others. We ought not to approach each other with a coldly calculating manner; we need mutual confidence and trust. Confidence is not blind, but it always commits itself beyond the measure of cool calculation. Love may be rewarded with ingratitude, confidence may be misplaced and disappointed.
