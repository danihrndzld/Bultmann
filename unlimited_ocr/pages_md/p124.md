<|det|>text [115, 32, 949, 461]<|/det|>I thank Thee that I am not like other men"? Let each of us examine himself to see if there is not in him also something of the Pharisaic pattern of life, in some form or other, in some degree or other! Are we truly in our words and deeds filled with the spirit of disinterested honesty and objectivity, of faithful devotion to our work and service? Or are our minds tainted too in some measure with this will to recognition? With the intention of standing forth as distinguished by what we have done? Do we not often eye others with the intention of comparing ourselves with them and fancying ourselves to be better and greater? Or do we eye them sometimes with tenseness and anxiety lest they should get in front of us? Is not our attitude towards others often envious and loveless? And are we not as a result oftentimes contentious and dogmatic? Do we not often deceive ourselves and fancy that we are better and greater than we are in fact? Do we never falsify the essence of the good that we like to do when we do it not from genuinely good motives but in order to fortify our self-conceit?
<|det|>text [115, 464, 949, 780]<|/det|>There is an infallible test of our dispositions in this matter. For the man who is truly free from the urge to self-assertion, from arrogance and ruthlessness, from envy and self-deception, feels himself free and secure in mixing with others; he does not get vexed when he has to stand back behind those who are better than himself, and it is with pleasure that he recognizes their superiority. It is of no consequence to him either when he stands behind others who only fancy themselves to be better than he and whose false pretentiousness he penetrates. They cannot affect him harmfully; he does not allow himself to be embittered, but is always cheerful and free from self-interested thoughts. Do we always feel ourselves to be so detached and inwardly secure, and if not how shall we gain such inner security?
