<|det|>text [78, 187, 898, 411]<|/det|>Thus it is those who wait upon God who are extolled in the first group of beatitudes. Four beatitudes follow which suggest how a genuine attitude of waiting bears fruit and must become a living power in man's life. For those who know how to wait upon God, the being of man is released from the present, from the given, from its connexion with what he has and is. It turns towards the future, towards what God will grant, what God will make of man. Thus man is released for all the encounters which God will send him and in which God's future will come to meet him.
<|det|>text [78, 413, 898, 682]<|/det|>“Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy.” This does not need many words of explanation; we need only point out that the word translated “merciful” has in truth a wider meaning; it implies not only compassion but (in general) goodness, active beneficent love. Blessed are they whose heart is inspired by such creative goodness. They shall obtain mercy. Yes, they are privileged to perceive the mercy of God active towards them in that they are allowed to experience how the man who is not embittered despite poverty, pain, and unfulfilled longing, the man who is readily accessible to the pain and sorrow of his neighbour—how such a one easily bears his own sorrows.
