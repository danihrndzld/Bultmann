For Christians it is a matter of course that a belief such as this violates the majesty of God, and the Christian recoils in horror from the consequences to which such a blasphemous doctrine leads. But these errors have often been denounced in our day and we do not purpose to enlarge on them. Nor do we need to dwell on the point that nature herself, just because God is present within her, ruthlessly corrects these errors. Whenever man forgets the confines which separate him from nature, when he tries to ascribe to his own creations the same value as that of natural force and growth, then nature reminds him ever afresh that these limits remain, and that the work of man's hand is weak and liable to decay.
<|det|>text [96, 350, 921, 621]<|/det|>Nature reminds him of this by the mere fact of the passing of time, of incessant change, through which nature ever renews herself, whereas man grows old and ceases to be. She reminds him of it again in that she repeatedly hurls herself on man's structures with destructive force in events which we call natural catastrophes, but which in truth are not catastrophes of nature at all but catastrophes for the proud edifices of man which violate the sphere of nature. For nature herself these events are only a powerful demonstration of her inviolable, irrefragable laws; for man and his works they are catastrophes, "for the elements hate the structures which man's hand has reared".
