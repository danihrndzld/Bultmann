His death is, as Scripture here states, His going away to the Father, and (to use a term which often occurs elsewhere in St. John's gospel) His glorification. In setting Him aside by crucifying Him, the world confirms His own word that He does not belong to it, and without its own knowledge and desire it must thus glorify Him. All this of course is only visible to one whose eyes the Spirit has opened, and who in consequence sees righteousness and victory where the world sees defeat and condemnation. Christianity too has often fallen a prey to the temptation to detect its triumphs in the sphere of the visible and to insist on this tangible evidence. Thus people speak of the influence of Christ on the course of world history, of the effects which His spirit has had on human manners and morals, of its uplifting and civilizing influence on nations. The world war showed little of all this, and, since it was a war between nominally Christian peoples, many so-called Christians and many pagans became bewildered about Christianity. Rightly so! For we are disastrously wrong when we wish to see the victory and righteousness of Christ manifested in the visible sphere. The world which does not apprehend His true victory answers of course: "Imagination! Fancy!" It does not know that what it declares to be imagination and fancy can be the strongest, most abiding of all realities, and that the things which it deems great and secure are but dreams which the breath of eternity disperses.
<|det|>text [96, 636, 925, 906]<|/det|>And finally the Spirit will convince the world of “judgment”. The world too speaks about judgment, and means thereby the judgment which world history pronounces, and which is embodied within the earthly sphere. But Scripture means something quite different: “of judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.” The world which fondly imagines that it is a closed circle, complete in itself, which supposes that it is responsible only to itself, and which subjects to its unconditioned claim all that belongs to it—this world as a whole is summoned before the judgment of God and is there judged, because it rejects the One who over against it voices the claim of the Eternal.
