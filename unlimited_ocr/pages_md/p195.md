<|det|>text [58, 48, 885, 295]<|/det|>For such as have experienced “that day”, prayer of this character is a matter of course. For these are they who have penetrated to that centre of life where they are alone in the presence of God, and this loneliness itself becomes for them a prayer. True prayer in the name of Jesus implies that with Him we enter consoledly the region of solitary encounter with God, in whose sight we are aware of standing and by whom we allow ourselves to be confronted. “In the world you have tribulation; but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world.” This solitariness of the soul can itself become a prayer, a cry of the heart for God:
<|det|>text [61, 297, 885, 475]<|/det|>"It may be that I pierce through rugged mountain sides, through harsh veins, like ore, alone; so deep I delve that I see no end, no distant view; all has become near, all nearness hard as stone. Yet I am not experienced in the ways of sorrow, and this entombing darkness makes me small. But Thou art; may the weight of Thy glory burst upon me, the weight of Thy whole hand smite me, and I with all my treasure fall prostrate before Thee."1
<|det|>text [61, 477, 885, 543]<|/det|>Of such prayer it is true to say: “In prayer we rise to Him as from the bands of death. Sorely our heart needed His breath which breathed around us.” \( ^{2} \)
<|det|>text [58, 545, 888, 701]<|/det|>Whoever practises such prayers goes back to the round of daily life, comforted and thankful that with all the impulses of his heart he may open himself to God without reserve. Such a one will be accompanied by the prayer of solitude in all life's encounters; it will impart to him that freedom and joy in the inspiration of which he will no longer need to ask questions.
